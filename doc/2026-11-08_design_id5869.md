## 1. The one idea everything follows from

The signed journal (#806) is the system of record; every backend
store is a materialisation of it. Backup, recovery, and exit are
therefore all the same operation at different speeds: **capture the
journal (plus a world-state snapshot as an accelerator), move it in
an open format, and replay it somewhere independent.** Provider
features may accelerate any step, but no step may _depend_ on a
provider feature — a recovery path that needs the provider you are
recovering from is not a recovery path.

## 2. Decision: point-in-time recovery is journal replay

1. **PITR target.** Any point in the retained window is recoverable
   at **journal-entry granularity**: recovery means "world state as
   of journal sequence _N_" (equivalently, the last entry at or
   before timestamp _T_). There is no coarser snapshot-only target.
2. **Retention window (contract requirement).** The journal is
   retained online for **at least 30 days**; world-state snapshots
   are taken **daily and retained 90 days**, and **weekly snapshots
   are retained 1 year**. An export bundle (§4) taken at any snapshot
   is retained independently of the provider for at least 1 year.
   #5868 prices this; implementation may exceed it, never undercut
   it without amending this record.
3. **Storage-contract hooks each backend must expose** (the backend-
   specific half of PITR, stated as #5863 contract requirements):
   - a **monotonic journal sequence** visible to readers, with a
     timestamp per entry;
   - **snapshot + delta read**: a consistent world-state snapshot at
     a named sequence, plus journal entries after it;
   - **replay-in**: append/replay of exported journal entries into an
     empty store, preserving sequence, timestamps, and signatures;
   - **schema/contract version** stamped on every snapshot and
     export, so a restore never silently crosses a version boundary.
4. **Provider-native PITR is an accelerator, never the mechanism.**
   D1 Time Travel, Turso point-in-time restore, and their successors
   may be used for operator convenience inside their windows, but
   drills (§6) must prove the journal-replay path works without
   them. A backend whose native PITR is the _only_ way to reach a
   point in the window fails this record.
5. **Restore shape.** Restore = provision an empty store behind the
   contract → load the newest snapshot at or before the target
   sequence → replay journal deltas to the target → run the §6
   verification before the store serves traffic. Restores are
   always to a fresh store, never an in-place rewind of the live
   one; the live store is evidence until the restore is verified.

## 3. Decision: what a backup is

A backup is an **export bundle (§4) taken on schedule**, not a
provider snapshot. Provider snapshots may exist as an operational
extra, but the daily/weekly artefacts that satisfy §2.2 are bundles
held outside the provider's control (separate account/bucket and,
for the weekly, separate provider or offline storage). A backup
that only the provider can read back is a copy, not a backup.

## 4. Decision: the open-format export bundle

1. **Shape.** A bundle is a directory (or single archive) containing:
   - `manifest` — contract/schema version, journal sequence range,
     snapshot sequence, per-file checksums, entry counts, and the
     journal signatures needed to verify provenance (#806);
   - `journal/` — journal entries in canonical newline-delimited
     JSON, in sequence order, split into numbered segments;
   - `snapshot/` — world state at the snapshot sequence, expressed
     in contract terms (the same form #5866 fixtures use), not as a
     backend-native database file;
   - `blobs/` — large payloads, content-addressed, referenced from
     journal/snapshot by hash;
   - `README` in-bundle — format version and the restore procedure,
     so the bundle is self-describing without this repo.
2. **Completeness guarantee.** Everything reachable through the
   #5863 contract — every journal entry, every world-state record,
   every blob — is in the bundle, byte-for-byte restorable, with
   **no lossy type mappings**: values are encoded in the journal's
   canonical types, never through a provider type that cannot
   round-trip (provider-specific rowids, timestamps without zone or
   precision, floats standing in for decimals are the canonical
   failures this rule exists to forbid).
3. **What a bundle deliberately does not contain:** derived indexes
   (rebuilt on restore), provider-internal replication state,
   credentials or secrets (referenced by name only, re-provisioned
   at restore), and uncommitted/in-flight writes (the bundle ends
   at a committed journal sequence, named in the manifest).
4. **Rejected alternatives:**
   - _Backend-native dumps as the canonical export_ (a D1 SQLite
     file, a sqld dump): they tie the exit to one backend's format,
     bypass journal invariants on re-import, and cannot be replayed
     into the other candidate backend — they are #5867 migration
     inputs at best, not this record's export.
   - _Provider snapshots as the backup:_ unreadable outside the
     provider, so they fail the exit requirement by construction.
   - _Continuous cross-provider replication as the exit strategy:_
     it pre-decides a standing second backend (a #5867/#5681 call),
     doubles the cost surface #5868 must price, and still needs this
     export for the day both replicas share a bug.

## 5. Decision: the provider-exit runbook

Exit is a rehearsed procedure (§6 drills), executed in this order:

1. **Declare and freeze.** Announce the exit; set the source store
   read-only at a named journal sequence _F_ (the exit point).
2. **Export.** Take a full bundle at _F_ (§4). Verify checksums and
   signatures against the manifest before touching anything else.
3. **Restore into the independent target.** Provision the target
   (the other backend, a self-hosted store, or local — independence
   from the exiting provider is the requirement) and restore per
   §2.5 to sequence _F_.
4. **Verify before cutover.** Replay-equivalence: target world state
   at _F_ matches the source's declared state at _F_ (the #5866
   oracle check); journal signatures verify; record counts and blob
   hashes match the manifest. Any mismatch aborts the exit with the
   source untouched.
5. **Cut over.** Point the edition at the target; run read traffic,
   then writes. The source stays read-only and intact.
6. **Point of no return.** Only after the target has served verified
   production traffic for a full snapshot cycle is the source
   eligible for teardown. **Teardown — or letting source-side
   retention lapse — is the point of no return**, and it is a
   separate, explicit action, never a step that happens by expiry
   during the exit window.
7. **After exit.** The exit bundle and the verification record are
   retained for at least 1 year; the exit is not "done" until a
   post-exit drill restores from the exit bundle alone.

## 6. Decision: restore verification and drills

- **Every restore verifies.** No restore — drill, recovery, or
  exit — is complete until replay equivalence and signature checks
  (§5.4) pass; a restore that "probably worked" is a failed restore.
- **Monthly:** automated restore drill — restore the latest daily
  bundle into an independent (non-provider or other-provider)
  target and run the verification. Drill failure is an incident,
  not a backlog item: PITR is broken until a drill passes.
- **Quarterly:** full exit rehearsal — §5 steps 1–5 against a
  scratch target, timed; the measured duration is recorded on
  #5869's successor tracking issue, because an exit that takes
  longer than the provider-risk window it protects against is a
  design input to #5681, not a surprise.
- **On change:** any change to the journal format, the contract
  version, or either backend adapter requires a passing drill
  before it ships (the drill is that change's regression test,
  with replay equivalence as its oracle).

## 7. Backend-specific parameters: decision rule, not a guess

The hooks in §2.3 are the contract; how each backend satisfies them
is settled mechanically once #5867 lands, and recorded as an
addendum to this record (the addendum may not weaken §2–§6):

- **D1:** sequence/timestamps from the journal tables the contract
  owns (never D1-internal bookmarks); snapshot+delta via contract
  reads; Time Travel usable only as a §2.4 accelerator.
- **Turso / libSQL:** the same contract-owned journal; native
  point-in-time features likewise accelerators only; embedded
  replica files are never the export form (§4.4).
- **Both:** each backend independently satisfies every hook and
  passes the same drill; "the other backend has it" is not coverage.

## 8. Invariants implementation must hold

- **E1 — Exportable without loss.** Every stored record is
  exportable in the §4 format without lossy types and restorable
  byte-for-byte; a type that cannot round-trip may not be admitted
  into storage through the contract.
- **E2 — Verifiable independence.** An exit is verifiable by
  restoring a bundle into a target independent of the source
  provider and proving replay equivalence — before teardown, every
  time.
- **E3 — No provider-only path.** No backup, recovery, or exit step
  depends on provider-only tooling; provider features accelerate,
  they never gate.
- **E4 — Journal is truth.** Snapshots and materialised state are
  accelerators; any conflict between a snapshot and the signed
  journal resolves in favour of the journal.
- **E5 — Frozen exit point.** An exit or PITR restore names its
  journal sequence; nothing after that sequence leaks into the
  restore, and nothing before it is missing.
- **E6 — Secrets by reference.** Bundles contain no credentials;
  a restore re-provisions secrets by name at the target.
- **E7 — Drills or it didn't happen.** Recovery capability exists
  only while the §6 drills pass; a lapsed or failing drill means
  the capability is reported as absent, not assumed.

## 9. What this record does not do

It does not choose or migrate the backend (#5867), define the
storage contract (#5863 — it states hooks the contract must
expose), set cost guardrails (#5868 — it states windows #5868
prices), or implement any backup/export/restore code. Implementation
issues, when filed, cite the invariant (§8) or step (§2/§5/§6) they
realise.
