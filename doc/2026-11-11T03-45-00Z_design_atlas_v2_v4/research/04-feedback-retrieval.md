## First: V2 must distinguish missing knowledge from buried knowledge

Build the evaluation and traces before learning. For a query and corpus snapshot, assemble independent relevance judgments from known-source discovery, lexical and semantic search, graph if available, and manual inspection. Record query, intended task, actor/domain, authorization ceiling, time, corpus/version, known relevant source and span identifiers, graded label and rationale. Resolve conflicting labels as scoped judgments, not universal truth. Separate cases:

1. **Corpus absent**: independently verified source exists but is absent from the authorized indexed snapshot; check ingestion, source reachability and expected freshness. If no independent source is known, call it *unknown*, not absent.
2. **Present but not eligible**: authorization, sensitivity, deletion or explicit filter excludes it; correct exclusion is not ranker failure. Evaluate only within the caller's permitted set.
3. **Eligible but candidate missed**: corpus has a judged span but no retriever produced it at the candidate depth, including approximate-index vs exact-scan miss.
4. **Candidate produced, ranked low or discarded**: record position and score at every stage, including fusion, rerank, deduplication and token packing. Distinguish rank cutoff from context-budget omission.
5. **Retrieved but answer failed**: evidence present in delivered context yet answer or citation fails; generation/attribution, not retrieval, needs work.

An assessor verifies source presence against an immutable snapshot and source manifest, not the system's own top-k. A synthetic exhaustively enumerated eligible corpus is the decisive missing-versus-buried oracle: positive controls prove presence and negative controls establish absence in that fixture, not across an unknowable real corpus. Track stage and coverage confidence independently. For disputed or incompletely judged cases mark unknown; pooling is incomplete and conventional metrics often count unjudged as irrelevant [TREC24]. Stratify failures by query type, source/tier, language, age, authorized corpus size, actor, snapshot and model/index revision. Paired comparisons use the same frozen query and corpus; report coverage and uncertainty, not only averages.

### V2 instrumentation and feedback contract

- Emit immutable event IDs, query/session or privacy-preserving join key, timestamps, applicable consent and tenant/security scope, corpus snapshot and content versions, model/pipeline/index/config revisions, query features subject to retention rules, allowed tiers, candidate source and rank, raw stage scores and score semantics, filtering/dedup reasons, final position, displayed/exposed vs merely eligible, truncation and context inclusion, outcome and latency/cost. Missing scores remain missing, never zero. Store only authorized detail; avoid recording secret snippets or raw query by default. ACL and sensitivity of derived traces must be at least that of their most sensitive input.
- Capture explicit positive/negative judgments linked to the **particular query, document version, stage and user**; allow correction, retraction and reasons such as irrelevant, stale, inaccessible, duplicate, wrong answer or missing evidence. Neither no click nor missing label is a negative. Feedback on generated answer is not automatically a document relevance label. Preserve disagreement and annotator provenance; use domain- or person-specific views rather than indiscriminate global learning.
- Separate immutable event collection from an opt-in, auditable feedback write plane: v1 service is read-only. Bound retention, deletion propagation and access; prevent feedback, model artifacts and logs from laundering sensitive content across tiers. A withheld or deleted source must not persist through training exports or caches without a governed deletion/retraining policy.
- Cold-start default is existing deterministic baseline, no personal history needed. Rate limit and authenticate feedback; separate trust levels and cap influence, audit coordinated attacks and suspicious bursts, hold out adversarial evaluations, and retain provenance to exclude compromised events. Never let a negative vote override access control or delete source material.

## Evaluating changes: oracles and gates

| Gate (thresholds provisional, chosen in advance) | Oracle and decision |
| --- | --- |
| Trace/diagnosis completeness | Synthetic corpus with known manifests, excluded documents and injected failures: exact expected stage and disposition; replay deterministic snapshots and compare event schema and ranks. Fail closed on inaccessible artifacts. |
| Candidate recall and rank | Independently judged, pooled, held-out relevance sets; report recall@candidate-depth, nDCG@k, MRR where appropriate, judged fraction and unknown cases, by slice. Baseline BM25, current dense exact scan, and hybrid; require precommitted paired improvement or non-inferiority and bounded regressions. TREC uses nDCG@10 with graded NIST labels [TREC20]; BEIR found BM25 robust across its benchmark, not proof for Atlas [BEIR]. |
| Index | Exact scan over the same model/snapshot/authorized tier is the oracle for ANN neighbor recall and result discrepancy; measure p50/p95/p99 latency, build/update time, memory, disk and sync, including filtered queries and stale/deleted data. Stay scan-first if index does not meet predefined quality and operational constraints. |
| Feedback | Seed known positive, negative, corrected, retracted and conflicting events; reference event-state model checks idempotency, authorization, retention, provenance and replay; inject poisoned identities and verify bounded impact. Human double-judging and adjudication provide a separate relevance oracle. |
| Policy and rollout | Shadow replay then randomized held-out online experiment with explicit quality, safety, cost, latency and cohort guardrails; compare with frozen baseline with confidence intervals. A kill switch restores the exact baseline config, compatible model/index/snapshot and caches; test restart and rollback. No causal claim from raw clicks. |

A benchmark alone cannot certify missing-corpus classification: sample discovery outside retrieval and explicitly document source collection limits. Split by time, domain and where feasible users/documents, preventing repeated versions and near duplicates leaking across train and test. Refresh judgments and maintain a frozen regression set. Annotate representativeness and disagreement, including minority users and rare domains.

## V2 → V3 → V4 options and dependencies

**V2: observability and baselines, not automatic training.** Implement stage-level explanations and opt-in scoped feedback; exact scan, lexical and dense independent runs, then rank fusion as a low-assumption comparator. Candidate and display logs enable the missing-vs-buried taxonomy. Compare rank-only RRF with calibrated weighted lexical/dense fusion on held-out judgments; neither is a universal winner. Bruch et al. found their convex combination beat RRF in their tested settings and RRF sensitive to parameters [Fusion], not that this transfers to Atlas. Judge calibration separately by tier and query class; raw BM25 and cosine distances are not commensurate probabilities. Document model, pipeline and index versions and score direction.

**V3: offline supervised learning, contingent on quality and volume.** Start with interpretable per-stage weights or pairwise learning-to-rank using explicit, scoped labels; compare against V2 baselines and a no-personalization model. Training labels are not independent if selected from shown results: expand pools and sample unseen candidates. Include human-reviewed hard negatives, not every unclicked candidate. Sparse individuals receive pooled/domain priors with opt-out and fallback; prevent globalizing personal preferences. Dataset snapshots, code, feature definitions, labels, consent, model and calibration versions are reproducible and revocable. Promote only after offline gates and shadow/online safety checks; support rollback to complete prior artifact bundle.

**V4: adaptive exploration only when V3 and logging prerequisites hold.** Options include bounded randomized swaps, contextual bandits choosing a ranker or retrieval budget, or adaptive tier/index search. Log actual action/slate probabilities, candidate set, policy version and reward window. Position bias means click propensity is not relevance [Joachims]; offline inverse-propensity or doubly robust estimation requires known propensities, overlap/support, correct assumptions and variance diagnostics [OPE]. A single-action estimator is not automatically valid for ranked slates. If support is zero or effective sample size inadequate, abstain from a causal estimate; exploration must obey authorization, safety and privacy constraints. Compare against a randomized holdout, not just historical clicks. Freeze and roll back policies on drift, cost or quality regression.

**Graph is an alternative, not a prerequisite.** Compare source-linked entity/edge expansion, graph as an additional candidate generator, and community-summary GraphRAG for global questions against lexical/dense baselines. GraphRAG reports improvements for global sensemaking on particular roughly million-token datasets [GraphRAG], not general Atlas retrieval. Measure incremental relevant evidence, citation fidelity, edge precision, staleness, ingestion/LLM cost and ACL propagation. Bound traversal fan-out; avoid using inferred edges or community summaries as verified facts. Preserve edge-to-source provenance and rebuild affected summaries on deletion.

**Cost-aware choices.** Measure end-to-end quality against latency, embedding/re-embedding tokens, index build and storage, retained versions, trace volume, sync and query cost. Compare fixed scan, indexed search and adaptive budget on the same query slices and snapshots. Estimate counterfactual cost using measured unit prices and resource usage, not only projected model-call spend. A policy that skips expensive search can hide relevant results: report recall and tail latency, including cold/rare cases. Compression, eviction, summaries and tiering require provenance, freshness and recoverability checks; no automatic deletion follows from a forecast.

## Dead ends and open decisions

Dead ends: training directly on uncorrected clicks; labeling all unclicked or unjudged material irrelevant; optimizing ranking to solve absent source ingestion; merging incompatible vector spaces by score normalization; treating ANN as lossless without exact oracle; assuming a learned preference applies to every user; using an LLM judge as sole ground truth; and allowing a policy to bypass sensitivity ceilings. Open decisions: feedback ownership and consent, deletion/retraining semantics, logical-to-physical tier migration, annotation budget and relevance granularity, baseline thresholds, admissible randomization and retention of diagnostic data. Each is a design decision, not established by these papers.
