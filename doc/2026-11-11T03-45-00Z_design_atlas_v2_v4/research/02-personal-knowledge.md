Research for Atlas #6020, 2026-11-11 UTC. This is option discovery, not an implementation commitment. All external links below were retrieved 2026-11-11; repository README and vendor documentation statements are **product claims**, not independent performance or security tests. No product was run. The parent's `gh issue view` snapshot in [07-issue-snapshot.json](07-issue-snapshot.json) supplies #6020's mandate: advance automatic ingestion, provenance, versioning, feedback, classification and the cross-source graph across v2–v4; it is issue evidence, not deployment evidence. ## Survey before recommendations

### Mem: notes as a low-friction agent corpus

Mem's [developer docs](https://docs.mem.ai/) and [MCP overview](https://docs.mem.ai/mcp/overview.md) describe hosted OAuth access for agents to search, read, create, edit, trash and restore notes and organize collections. [Search API](https://docs.mem.ai/api-reference/notes/search-notes.md) documents metadata and optional full markdown, time and collection filters, deterministic snapshot pagination and a **100-result bounded relevance window**, not exhaustive enumeration; list separately for export. The [API index](https://docs.mem.ai/llms.txt) documents version-checked full-body updates, permanent delete vs trash, and related-note indexing lag. These are documented contracts, not observed behavior.

Adoption lesson: let people keep working in notes while an agent uses the same corpus; search snippets first, expand on demand. Limitation: hosted OAuth is not an offline or fine-grained authority guarantee; collections are organization, not demonstrated ACLs. Its [security guidance](https://docs.mem.ai/mcp/security-best-practices.md) advises trusted clients, dedicated workspaces, destructive-write confirmation and treating prompt content as untrusted. Transfer separate read/write scopes and bounded-search disclosure; do not copy a universal write-capable agent token or mistake ranked search for a complete backup.

### Rewind and the capture lifecycle

Rewind illustrates passive retrieval value and a **dead end as a current capture dependency**, not a product recommendation. The [Limitless help article](https://help.limitless.ai/en/articles/13048802-where-can-i-find-my-rewind-data) locates its local database and explicitly says there is no supported API. Its [uninstall guide](https://help.limitless.ai/en/articles/13024699-how-do-i-delete-uninstall-rewind) makes deleting recordings a separate optional action: uninstall is not erasure. The [primary Limitless CEO announcement](https://www.limitless.ai/) explicitly announces Rewind's sunset. [Contemporaneous reporting reproducing the company announcement](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/) gives 2025-12-19 for disabling capture and reports regional Limitless account deadlines; the retrieved primary page's FAQ answers were not visible in our page extraction, so that exact date remains secondary-reported, not independently verified here. Do not conflate Rewind local data with Limitless cloud data or assume continued recovery.

Mechanism is passive screen/audio capture and retrieval; the concrete lifecycle failure is that local bytes alone do not guarantee a documented, importable format or surviving capture software. Adoption value: effortless capture remembers forgotten context. Costs: consent of bystanders, privileged screen contents, passwords, transcription/OCR error, retention, device load, access after vendor shutdown and disclosure of recordings to agents. Transfer only explicit opt-in capture, visible pause/exclusions, narrow time/app selection, verified export and independently readable archives. Alternatives: user-selected documents or local capture indexed on device. Do not promise that simply uninstalling deletes, that a cloud export includes the local archive, or that a private database can be imported.

### Screenpipe: local-first capture alternative

The [Screenpipe repository README](https://github.com/screenpipe/screenpipe) describes accessibility-tree capture with OCR fallback, transcription, app/window filters, optional at-rest encryption, a local search/MCP interface and offline capture after setup. It explicitly distinguishes local storage from cloud AI, sync and managed team storage. It reports approximately 5–20% CPU and 0.5–3 GB RAM for capture, dependent on settings; storage must be measured on a representative device. License is source-available; inspect its terms before reuse. These are maintainers' claims, not benchmark results or proof all filters are comprehensive.

Transfer the separate capture/index/inference data paths and candid operational sizing; don't equate local-first with no exfiltration, universal consent, encrypted backups, or reliable secret detection. Compare to explicit ingest: less friction and richer time context versus substantially more sensitive data and an ongoing device/service burden. A capture source should come with app/window/time exclusions, permission and pause state, deletion propagation, and egress policy before agent access.

### Khoj: self-hosted personal RAG

[Khoj's repository README](https://github.com/khoj-ai/khoj) describes self-hosted or hosted search over PDF, markdown, org-mode, Word and other sources; local or online models; Obsidian, Emacs, browser and other clients; and agents with custom knowledge and tools. Product claims, not verified deployment behavior. It demonstrates lowering adoption cost by integrating existing user surfaces instead of requiring a new knowledge silo. A self-hosted service with a cloud model is not necessarily private end-to-end; model selection, connectors and network paths matter. Transfer configurable corpus/model boundaries and familiar clients; do not assume semantic search determines truth, licensing permission, or reproducible citations. Compare Mem's hosted simplicity against operator-controlled deployment and maintenance. AGPL-3.0 is a reuse constraint, not a security guarantee.

### Quivr: stream-to-citable-search engine

The [Quivr repository README](https://github.com/QuivrHQ/quivr), currently redirected to The Vibe Company, describes a different current product from historical 'second brain' discussions: API-first ingestion, PostgreSQL/object storage, asynchronous plugins, rebuildable index, hybrid search, exact passage/document/version pointers, withdrawal, alerting and MCP. It claims acknowledgment after durable storage. Search and alerts have separate lifecycle behavior. This is a particularly useful contrast to a monolithic chat UI: source version and evidence remain first-class while multiple clients use the same engine.

The vendor's [known limits](https://docs.quivr.thevibecompany.co/reference/known-limits) specify text only/no OCR, 256 KiB and 64 indexed pieces per version with its first-party plugin, 50 results with no pagination, 64 concurrent searches per API process, and about one million news articles run on one installation while ten million per collection is unmeasured. An oversized version can be stored but not indexed, with a receipt. Old alert evidence expires after 30 days by default; webhooks can duplicate and stop retrying, while matches remain readable. API keys belong to an organization; applications supply user authorization. Meaning-alert processing can send article text to an external provider. These are disclosed vendor limits, not Atlas capacity figures.

Transfer durable-acceptance vs indexed readiness, explicit partial-index receipts, passage-level version citations, independently rebuildable derived indexes and bounded searchable status. Do not transfer indefinite historical retention over a deletion/right-to-purge obligation, or treat org API keys as end-user ACLs. Alternative: index-only local personal RAG is simpler but loses durable version/evidence semantics.

### Mem0: agent memory, distinct from source corpus

The [Mem0 repository README](https://github.com/mem0ai/mem0) describes user/session/agent memory and search, self-hosting or managed service. Its April 2026 algorithm description claims add-only extracted facts, entity linkage, fused semantic/BM25/entity ranking and temporal reasoning. It says benchmarks are for the managed platform, with proprietary optimizations unavailable in the OSS SDK: they are vendor-reported, not evidence of Atlas performance or equivalent local deployment. Default examples use external OpenAI models and embeddings. Agent-authored observations have equal weight in its described algorithm: convenient, but a serious authority/confabulation risk for Atlas.

Transfer typed, dated memory candidates with links to supporting observations and correction feedback, not automatic promotion into canonical facts. Distinguish human assertion, quoted third-party text, system observation, agent hypothesis and verified outcome; preserve conflicts and temporal validity. Scope by user, agent and task with actual authorization, not just filter parameters. Compare simple notes with generated summaries: extraction lowers retrieval costs but can lose qualifiers, provenance and contradict earlier facts. ADD-only is not a substitute for privacy deletion, correction, or supersession; derived facts must also be revoked or recomputed when underlying evidence is purged.

### Open WebUI: self-hosted interaction layer

[Open WebUI's README](https://github.com/open-webui/open-webui) describes an offline-capable self-hosted interface, local or cloud model connections, and a companion incremental sync for folders, repositories, buckets and wikis. Its branding-preservation license differs from a permissive OSS grant. Useful as a client/adoption comparison, not proof of source permissions, deletion semantics or citation correctness. It underlines that a user interface and a source-of-truth corpus are separable; an Atlas integration need not become another mandatory chat application.

## Cross-cutting candidates and boundaries

1. **Separate authority from relevance.** Agent-facing search returns source ID, immutable version, passage locator, timestamps, classification, authorization decision, evidence kind and uncertainty; rank only authorized candidates. Retrieved text is data, never instructions. Agent-generated claims remain attributed and corrigible, not equal to policy or owner directives merely because semantically similar.
2. **Feedback as evidence.** Capture explicit useful/irrelevant, correction, disputed source, stale and privacy flags against result/version and query context; support inspect/undo and evaluate changes offline. Feedback can be wrong or sensitive, and must not silently change access or convert popularity to authority. Measure retrieval recall, stale results, citation accuracy and false exposure separately.
3. **Portability and deletion are end-to-end.** Export source bytes plus manifest, version/lineage, classifications, consent, annotations and machine-readable provenance, then test restoration without the vendor; say what is omitted. Tombstone immediately for serving and propagate purge through chunks, vectors, caches, generated memories, replicas, exports and backups under explicit policy, recording completion without retaining forbidden content. Revocation of already-delivered external copies is not guaranteed.
4. **Scale from explicit budgets, not vendor scores.** Track source count, change rate, capture bytes/day, indexed coverage, backlog age, query p50/p95, cost, model version and privacy-boundary egress; benchmark representative corpus and failure/rebuild paths. Report stored-but-not-searchable items and freshness. Mem's bounded search and Quivr's index caps show why export and retrieval are distinct.
5. **Tradeoff to resolve, not a settled plan:** selective import with local retrieval is simpler and safer; continuous capture buys recall at higher privacy and operational cost; managed memory buys convenience while adding vendor and model egress dependence. Offer these as separately governed source classes rather than treating all personal material as one equally authoritative knowledge graph.

## Fit with Atlas v1 documents

The local [version/retention design](../../2026-11-08T10-26-00Z_design_atlas_content_versioning_retention/2026-11-08T10-26-00Z_design_atlas_content_versioning_retention.md) specifies stable file and immutable version IDs, source coordinates and lineage; the [deletion propagation design](../../2026-11-09T00-42-59Z_design_atlas_deletion_propagation.md) distinguishes confirmed disappearance, rights-triggered purge and tombstones. Personal capture and generated memories should extend, not silently replace, those boundaries. In particular, keep-all/history and purge have different triggers; never infer a source's legal rights from similarity or possession. These are design records, not proof of current implementation.
