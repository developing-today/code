## 1. Request

Drew's directive (2026-11-12, quoted in #9148 §1): a highly trusted server is taken over, or waits until it is trusted, and starts behaving badly, including tampering with search results. Acknowledgments should make lying expensive: if A answers "don't have it" while B produces the recipient list showing the data was sent to A, one of them is provably wrong, and defeating detection needs collusion. All transfers are signed and all signatures are always validated. The note must say what is logged, what is provable, and what the response playbook is when a contradiction is found, with a recommendation the sibling designs can build on and open questions recorded rather than settled silently.

**Verified when:** this note is merged under `doc/`, linked from #9108 with a comment, and each claim in §4–§6 cites a merged slice and test (or is marked as a gap). The sibling issues #10266 and #10279 can then take §7's slices as their contract without reopening §5's proof table.

## 2. Short answer

1. **The worked example needs one correction to be a proof.** B's recipient list is B's own statement, so on its own "B says it sent X to A, A says it does not have X" is a dispute, not a proof: either could be lying. What turns it into proof is **A's own signed `accepted` (or `retained_until`) receipt**, which B19 already issues to the sender (`recipient_key` is B's key). A later signed `Complete` answer from A over a scope that contains X, inside the receipt's retention interval and under the same policy, contradicts A's earlier signature. Both artifacts are signed by A's key, so any third party can check them without trusting B or the querier. That is the provable case. Everything else is `disputed` or `insufficient_evidence`.
2. **What is provable is narrow on purpose.** `accepted` proves admission at a time, not that A still holds X. A "don't have it" is only contradicted by a retention commitment (`retained_until`) or a policy that forbids deletion, and only when A claimed completeness. An unstated or partial answer proves nothing. A signed deletion (tombstone) under policy is a lawful answer, reported as `deleted_by_policy`, not `missing`.
3. **Injection is already closed; omission is the hard case.** The B32 gate refuses unsigned or wrongly signed data on every ingress path, and B17 forbids any hop from answering in the responder's name, so injected results cannot carry their claimed source's signature. Omission leaves no cryptographic trace unless the responder made a completeness claim, which is why completeness claims must be explicit, scoped and signed. Reordering is not provable at all; the defence is that the querier ranks results itself.
4. **Collusion is the residual.** A lying holder escapes detection only if every party holding one of its receipts for that object withholds it. The security parameter is the number of independent receipt holders (senders, plus witnesses if added). One honest holder suffices.
5. **A contradiction does not identify the culprit.** A self-contradiction proves that a key A controls signed both statements. It cannot tell an operator's lie from a leaked key. So the response routes through B28's existing incident declaration, where an undetermined cause already gets the takeover response, and never auto-declares a takeover.

## 3. Threat model

| Id  | Adversary                                         | Capability                                                       | Goal                                                    |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| T1  | Outsider holding a leaked key of an honest server | Signs anything under that key until revocation                   | Inject data, deny holdings, backdate                    |
| T2  | Takeover of a trusted server                      | Full control of the server and all its keys                      | Same as T1, plus suppress receipts it holds as a sender |
| T3  | Sleeper                                           | Behaves until trusted (high B15 priority, B31 class), then as T2 | Exploit accumulated trust                               |
| T4  | Colluding set                                     | Several servers coordinate                                       | Suppress or fabricate evidence                          |

Misbehaviours in scope (S1.110): **denial** ("don't have it"), **omission** from search results, **injection** of results not signed by their claimed source, **reordering** or ranking manipulation, **staleness** (answering from an old state), and **backdating** (claiming a signing time before compromise).

Out of scope: availability attacks (covered by B16 budgets and B15 timeouts), and anything that requires breaking Ed25519.

## 6. What is provable

The evaluator compares exactly two kinds of signed statement about the same digest. All statements are verified with B05 signer binding first, so a valid signature under an unbound key is an identity failure, not evidence.

| Evidence held                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Proves                                     | Does not prove                                                     | Evaluator result                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A's `accepted` receipt for digest X at t0                                                                                                                                                                           | A's gate admitted X at t0 under policy P   | That A still holds X at any later time                             | (no contradiction alone)                                                                                     |
| A's `retained_until(t2)` receipt for X, and A's signed `Complete` response at t1 (t0 ≤ t1 ≤ t2) over a scope containing X, same policy P, X absent from the results, and no signed tombstone for X from A before t1 | A's key signed two incompatible statements | Whether the operator lied or the key leaked; when the key leaked   | `self_contradiction`                                                                                         |
| A's `accepted` receipt for X (no retention), and A's `Complete` response omitting X                                                                                                                                 | Admission then absence                     | That A was obliged to keep X; deletion may be lawful               | `insufficient_evidence` (unless A's signed policy P forbids deletion; see Q2)                                |
| A's `verified_present` receipt at t1 and A's `Complete` response at t1' ≈ t1 omitting X                                                                                                                             | A held X at t1                             | Holding at t1' if t1' ≠ t1 and no retention                        | `self_contradiction` only if t1' falls inside a `retained_until` interval; otherwise `insufficient_evidence` |
| B's recipient-list commitment naming A, and A's denial                                                                                                                                                              | B claims it sent X to A                    | That A received it                                                 | `disputed`; resolved by A's receipt if B holds one                                                           |
| B's recipient list naming A, and no receipt from A                                                                                                                                                                  | Nothing about A                            | Anything                                                           | `insufficient_evidence`; B should not have counted A as a recipient                                          |
| A's `Unstated` or `Partial` response omitting X                                                                                                                                                                     | Nothing                                    | Anything                                                           | `insufficient_evidence` (S1.113: omission needs a completeness claim)                                        |
| Any response whose scope, policy digest or interval differs from the receipt's                                                                                                                                      | Nothing comparable                         | Anything                                                           | `insufficient_evidence`                                                                                      |
| A's denial and A's signed tombstone for X before the denial                                                                                                                                                         | Lawful deletion                            |                                                                    | `deleted_by_policy`, not a contradiction                                                                     |
| A result in a response not signed by its claimed source                                                                                                                                                             | Injection attempt by whoever supplied it   | Who that was, unless the session binding proves the supplying peer | Refused at the gate; B06 records it; attributed only per the versioned design's attribution rule             |
| A response ordering that disagrees with the querier's own ranking                                                                                                                                                   | Nothing                                    | Manipulation                                                       | Not evaluated; the querier re-ranks locally                                                                  |
| A `Complete` response at t1 omitting X, where X was admitted by A at t0 < t1 under retention                                                                                                                        | Staleness or omission (indistinguishable)  | Which one                                                          | `self_contradiction` (same as row 2)                                                                         |

The evaluator returns `self_contradiction` only when object and digest, scope (the scope must provably contain the digest), interval, policy digest, authorization and claim semantics all match, as the versioned design requires. Any missing match is `insufficient_evidence`. Withheld evidence is never proof of omission.

Two properties make the positive case sound:

- **No trust in the accuser.** Both statements are signed by the accused key. The accuser only supplies bytes; it cannot forge either one.
- **No reliance on wall clocks across signers.** Both times (t0, t1, t2) come from the same signer's own signed fields, so the comparison is about that signer's consistency with itself. A backdated response still contradicts a receipt whose retention interval covers the backdated time. Cross-signer timing (needed for staleness against a third party's data) stays `insufficient_evidence` unless a witness supplies independent time, as B02 and B28 already rule.

## 7. Recommendation: next implementable slices

These are the contracts the sibling issues can build on. Each is small and testable on its own.

### 7.1 Recipient-list commitment (part of B29, #10266)

`RecipientCommitment { transfer_id, digest, recipients: either an exact sorted key list or a Merkle root, issued_at, policy_digest, signer, signature }`, plus an inclusion proof for the root form. It is the sender's own statement and is only ever `disputed` evidence on its own. Its value is that it tells an auditor which receipts the sender should be able to produce: a sender that lists A but holds no receipt from A has itself made an unsupported claim. A sender must not list a recipient whose `accepted` receipt it did not obtain; the list is then a summary of receipts held, not a separate truth.

### 7.2 Contradiction evaluator (B29, #10266)

A pure function over verified artifacts:

```text
evaluate(receipt: VerifiedReceipt, response: VerifiedResponse, tombstones: &[VerifiedTombstone])
    -> SelfContradiction | Disputed | DeletedByPolicy | InsufficientEvidence(reason)
```

The reason enumerates which match failed (digest not in scope, policy differs, interval outside retention, completeness not `Complete`, signer key differs, binding failed). The test oracle is the one already written into #10266: positive only when every field matches, otherwise `insufficient_evidence`, checked against an independent reference model.

### 7.3 Typed scope and tombstones (prerequisite gaps)

- **Gap 1: B17's `SignedResponse.scope` is opaque bytes.** The evaluator cannot decide "this scope contains digest X" for an arbitrary byte string. Recommend a typed scope for completeness claims: exact digest lookup, or an object id with a version set. `Complete` is only meaningful under a typed scope; under an opaque scope it is treated as `Unstated` by the evaluator.
- **Gap 2: B19 has no signed deletion.** The versioned design requires a signed scoped tombstone and a separate `deleted_by_policy` answer. Without it, an honest holder that deleted X under policy could look like a liar. Recommend a fourth signed statement kind, kept out of `Assertion` because it is not a receipt: `Tombstone { digest, scope, deleted_at, policy_digest, signer, signature }`.
- **Gap 3: B17 responses carry `generated_at` but no explicit as-of interval.** Recommend binding the store epoch the answer was computed from, so staleness against the responder's own receipts is comparable.

### 7.4 Search integrity contract (B30, #10279)

- Each result in a `SignedResponse` is either a full signed envelope that the querier passes through its own B32 gate, or a digest that the querier fetches and gates. A result that fails the gate is dropped and recorded in B06; it never counts toward completeness.
- `Complete` is allowed only under a typed scope (7.3).
- The querier ranks results itself from verified content; remote order is advisory and never trusted, so reordering has no effect rather than needing detection.
- Cross-checking: for scopes the querier cares about, it may ask a second responder or challenge with `verified_present`. That is a mitigation for omission without completeness claims, not a proof.

### 7.5 Playbook hook (B28 plus B25)

A `self_contradiction` finding is an input to existing machinery, not a new verdict path. See §8.

## 8. Response playbook when a contradiction is found

1. **Record.** Store the evidence bundle (the exact signed receipt and response bytes, any tombstones considered, and the evaluator's result) locally. Nothing leaves the node by default.
2. **Contain locally, automatically and reversibly.** Lower A's B15 priority for the affected scope and mark A's answers in that scope as `Indeterminate` for the querier's purposes. Count the finding as one attributable event under the versioned design's thresholds; three distinct attributable events for A within 10 minutes trigger local quarantine and review. Retries of one event count once.
3. **Escalate to an operator.** Raise the finding for the operator. The operator decides whether to declare a B28 incident, and of which kind. The finding alone never declares a takeover, because it cannot distinguish an operator's lie from a leaked key (T1 vs T2). If the operator declares and cannot tell the cause, B28's `undetermined` already gets the takeover response with an indeterminate cause.
4. **Once declared, B28 runs as built.** Immediate revocation from the suspected start, blast radius by admission time, suspect data quarantined, prior data flagged, historical validity `Indeterminate` until revalidated by an independent witness or a fresh attestation by an unrevoked key that is not the server's.
5. **Inform affected users locally.** Users whose queries were answered by A in the affected scope see that those answers are indeterminate.
6. **Optional export.** With operator opt-in, publish the finding as a B27 `Claim` with redacted public metadata and access-controlled evidence references. A gets the equal right of reply that B27 requires. Publication proves only that the claim was made; it does not change anyone else's reputation automatically.
7. **Recover.** Revalidated records are released. Priority recovers by the versioned design's hysteresis rule, and positive service evidence never overrides a security quarantine.

## 9. Collusion residual (S1.105–S1.107)

Receipts flow to the sender. So for A's denial of X to go undetected, **every** party holding an A-signed receipt for X must withhold it. If X reached A from k independent senders, the colluding set is A plus all k. If X reached A from one sender B, the colluding set is {A, B}; B could also simply never have sent X and fabricate nothing, because a recipient list without A's receipt is worthless.

The stated security parameter is therefore **the number of independent holders of A's receipt for X**. Ways to raise it, in order of cost:

1. **Anti-entropy gossip of receipts.** Senders share receipts they hold with peers that also hold X (B09 already exchanges inventories). This multiplies holders without new trust.
2. **A witness.** The sender countersigns the receipt into a witness log (B27, or a transparency log). Suppression then needs the witness too. Recommended for high-value scopes, not by default.
3. **Challenge sampling.** Any holder of X can issue `verified_present` challenges to A at random; a refusal or a wrong response is itself signed evidence.

Collusion can raise the cost of lying from one server to a set; it cannot make lying impossible. That residual is accepted by analysis, as S1.106 states.
