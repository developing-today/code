# Design review after the spikes

- Date: 2026-10-11 (UTC 08:08)
- Scope: the whole design in the [parent document](./2026-10-11T03-44-33Z_design_groups_events_delegation.md), measured against the six Phase 1 spikes, the [research findings](./2026-10-11T04-20-17Z_research_findings.md), and the owner decisions of 2026-10-11, including the 1,000 tier.
- Status: review. Recommendations in §6 need owner decisions. No product code changed.
- Evidence: spike code, results, and logs are in [spikes/](./spikes/README.md). Each spike's `RESULTS.md` is the source for its numbers.
- The parent document stays as written. Where this review differs from it, §4 lists the difference.

## 1. Verdict

The design holds. All six exit criteria pass, with the limits in §10. Five results change the design:

1. **Membership needs a shim.** p2panda-auth's StrongRemove works, but only through an auth-frontier mapping (each auth operation names the maximal auth operations in its full causal past) and an admission gate. The raw `after` frontier stalls with `MissingDependencies`. A naive mapping voids a valid add.
2. **Dependents are voided by authority loss, not by closure.** Design §6.2 rule 3 is wrong. p2panda voids a dependent operation only when its author lost authority through the voided operation. An independent manager's dependent add stands. Closing over all dependents is an owner decision (§6, item 1).
3. **`from_join` is feasible, with conditions.** It needs an immediate `update` after the add, withholding of pre-join application messages, and a membership fix in our DGM. Upstream `dcgka.rs` overwrites the joiner's membership.
4. **PCS is narrower than the design says.** Rotation heals removed members and anyone who holds only the group secret. It does not heal a member device whose own keys are compromised. The only verified fix is remove and re-add.
5. **Sync and storage are settled.** Range reconciliation costs about 6–7 KB over 7–8 round trips at 1,000–10,000 chains. LogSync costs 76 KB and 760 KB over 2 round trips. redb beats SQLite on this workload and avoids a dependency conflict with p2panda-store. We do not need p2panda-sync, p2panda-stream, or p2panda-store.

One more result settles an operational choice (§3.6). A relay we run on our own host, plus tickets, works without n0. That answers the N0 question.

## 3. The design, layer by layer

What we would build. Each item notes the spike that decided it.

### 3.1 Events

- **Header.** p2panda-core's `Header`, with a `ChainExt` extension holding `kind`, an optional `group`, `after` (at most 32 hashes), and the domain tag `id.events.v1`. Sequence base 0. S1 found that base 1 forces a phantom backlink onto the first event.
- **Verification.** Decode from bytes only. `AnyHeader::decode` verifies signatures. Never use typed `verify()`, `validate_header` on in-memory headers, or the cached `hash()`. On receipt we check `MAX_AFTER`, reject unknown extension keys, require canonical re-encoding, and run `verify_strict` over the re-encoded bytes (S1, S5).
- **Encoding.** cbor-core =0.10.1 with the canonical form in §2. The body is kind-specific CBOR. The operation ID is the BLAKE3 hash of the encoded header, including the signature.

### 3.2 Storage

- redb 4.3.0 with one writer, the serving node. Tables: events by ID; chains keyed by (author, seq), with the uniqueness check in the same write transaction; fork proofs; waiting events; index tables.
- Waiting events are capped per author and expire. S2 showed that refused events and stalls make this a requirement (§5).
- The index can be rebuilt from events. Corruption costs time, not data.

### 3.3 Sync

- Range reconciliation over (author, seq), on a new ALPN `/id-sync/1`. The items are the events themselves, so event bytes move in the same exchange.
- LogSync is not used. It is cheaper only below about 35–40 chains with two new events, or when the diff is large. S1 measured 200 new events on 1,000 chains at 312 KB by range against 76 KB by LogSync. Range needs 7–8 round trips against LogSync's 2. We accept that trade for bytes at scale.
- The S1 range protocol is a simulation. Running it on real stores is S7 (§9).

### 3.4 Membership, delegation, and friendship

- **Membership.** p2panda-auth's StrongRemove. `dependencies` are derived on receipt from the verified `after` closure (the auth-frontier shim). Stored dependencies are not trusted.
- **Admission gate.** An operation with a missing auth dependency waits. An operation for a group absent at its dependencies is rejected. Every event our layer refuses becomes a placeholder no-op in the auth graph, so its descendants do not stall (S2 P7).
- **Friendship.** The two-member cap is ours. p2panda-auth's `ignore` set cannot carry it, so the cap is an ingest rewrite that turns an over-cap add into a no-op. Dissolve is a projection: an effective removal of either member ends the friendship. The spike's cap is a device. Production keeps the signed event and derives the rewrite.
- **Delegation.** Edges, attenuation at creation, chain search (any intact chain from the owner, the UCAN rule), a veto on a later action with no intact chain, and the three modes. Cascade is the default. Transfer is derived from the removal's successor field. The removal is signed, so the transfer is signed too (S2).
- **Void reasons**, recorded per event: "voided: concurrent with R", "voided: author lost authority through Z", "vetoed: no intact chain", and "refused: missing dependency" with the hash.

### 3.5 Encryption and joiner history

- **Keys.** p2panda-encryption DataScheme for every group by default. `scheme: none` is the explicit opt-out.
- **DGM.** Ours. Membership at a causal position comes from p2panda-auth. The DGM must insert the joiner on welcome, because the upstream path overwrites it (S3).
- **Ordering.** Our adapter names heads, not the per-author latest. When heads would exceed 32, publish a merge update naming up to 32 of them (S3 d). The heads orderer is untested (S10).
- **from_join.** The welcome carries an empty bundle, through the public API in about 20 lines. Then an immediate `update`. The joiner withholds pre-join application messages. Without withholding, the welcome fails and the joiner's state is lost (S3 c1).
- **full (default).** The welcome carries every epoch secret. Each costs about 146 B in CBOR today (S3 b). The cost is linear in the number of rotations. About 1,000 rotations give a welcome of about 146 KB. The upstream `serde_bytes` fix would cut that to roughly 40 B per secret (an S3 estimate). This is the largest cost of the default. §6 item 5 covers it.
- **PCS.** Rotation heals a leaked group secret, and it locks out removed members. It does not heal a member device whose own keys are compromised. For that, remove and re-add (S3 a).
- **Pre-keys.** The crate's default lifetime is 84 days (S3 e). spaces configures 90 days. Existing sessions survive expiry. First contact with an expired bundle fails with `KeyBundlesExpired`.

### 3.6 Transport, relays, and discovery

Three configurations, with trade-offs measured or read from iroh 1.3.0 source:

| Configuration                | Relays              | Lookup by node ID                           | Third parties see                    | Works behind NAT                                                |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| N0 preset                    | n0's relays         | Yes, through n0's DNS and pkarr             | Node-ID lookups, and relayed traffic | Yes, through n0's relays                                        |
| Minimal, direct only         | None                | No. A ticket must carry a reachable address | Nothing                              | Only where the address is reachable (public IP or port forward) |
| Minimal, our relays, tickets | Ours (`iroh-relay`) | No                                          | Our relay's operator                 | Yes, through our relay                                          |

What the spike showed (S1). A ticket dial on localhost succeeds in 1.2 ms. A ticket with no address fails at once with "No address lookup configured". A dead address takes 30 s to time out. A relay-only connection through a local HTTP relay succeeded with a ticket that has no direct addresses and no n0 service configured.

What the presets are. Minimal has no relays and no lookups. N0 is Minimal plus n0's lookups plus n0's relays. Under Minimal a node can be reached only at an address you already hold, and a peer behind NAT cannot connect without a relay.

Recommendation: Minimal, with our own relays on home servers, and tickets for every introduction. Bare node-ID lookup stays off by default. If the owner wants it, it is an explicit per-command opt-in, and the output names n0. Relays stay optional in the earlier sense: a direct address works without one, and our relay is only the fallback for NAT.

Decision needed (§6 items 2 and 3).

### 3.7 Payloads

- iroh-blobs =0.103.1, which is not yanked (S4). The research note's yank range was wrong (§5).
- The body hash equals the blob hash at every tested size. The blob ID is the body hash, with no mapping table.
- A fetch needs the provider's EndpointId and an address. A hash alone is not a route, because there is no content routing.
- Gating: EventMask in Intercept mode for `get` and `get_many`. A denied request resets the stream with code 1. Clients must read the code, or a denial looks like absence.
- `observe` is not gated. Its default mode serves requests, so existence and size can leak even when the bytes are gated (§6 item 4).
- Verified streaming works at 16 KiB leaves. One bad byte discards its leaf.
- Inline payloads go up to 64 KiB (`MAX_DOCUMENT_BYTES`). Larger payloads are blobs. The blob size is checked against policy before the fetch.

### 3.8 Tags, documents, index, and search

- Tags are events with observed-remove semantics (design §9.1). Not spiked. S8 covers the dots, the per-author clock, and the precedence against strong remove.
- The index keeps the α/Ω layout in redb. Prefix queries use an explicit upper bound. The tuple encoder needs the fix in §5 before it is reused.
- Searches: by subject, by name and value, and by author. Multi-term AND, as today.
- Documents: inline CBOR, or a blob reference. One form per document (design §9.3).

### 3.9 Invitations

- The Seitan V2 derivations are verified against keybase/client source (research §8.1): the 30-symbol alphabet, scrypt with N=2^10, r=8, p=1, the HMAC-SHA512 invite-ID truncation, and the seed derivation. The test vector is `4uywza+b3cga7rd6yc` giving `6303ec43bd61d21edb95a433faf06227`.
- Our adaptation adds a freshness check on `ctime` in the admin path. Keybase's V2 lacks it, and its V3 has it.
- Tickets give reachability only. Bearer links put the secret in the URL fragment, as Keybase does.
- Not implemented or spiked. S9.

### 3.10 Pull and metadata

- Pull is open by default, and configurable to `authorized`. Authorized pull is advisory. It stops honest nodes from serving unauthorised peers. It does not stop re-serving, does not recall copies, and does not hide metadata.
- Metadata visible to any storing node: author key, sequence, kind, group, `after` hashes, sizes, and timing. Bodies are encrypted in end-to-end groups and readable in `scheme: none` groups.
- This is the documented limit the owner asked for.

### 3.11 Time

Deferred. The correction in design §12 stands: a witness proves existence at or before the signed time.

## 5. Issues found

- **Tuple prefix bug (current code).** `pkgs/id/src/tuple.rs` escapes a NUL byte as `00 FF` and ends each field with a single `00`. An exact-match prefix for `a`, meaning the bytes `… a 00`, therefore also matches the field `a\0b`, which encodes as `… a 00 FF … 00`. S6 found it. Reading the escape and terminator code (lines 54–70 and 205–240) confirms it. `tags.rs` builds its index with this encoder today, so a value containing NUL can match an unrelated query. Fix: end each field with `00 00`, which cannot occur inside escaped data. Nested tuples use the same marker and need the same review. Add S6's regression cases. Priority: Phase 0.
- **Pages not byte-capped (current code).** Known from earlier. `PAGE_LIMIT` counts items, and 100 × 64 KiB exceeds the 1 MiB frame. Priority: Phase 0.
- **Welcome growth.** See §3.5. The parent design did not account for it. It makes `full` costly for groups with heavy churn. Priority: decide before Phase 5.
- **Placeholder admission.** Refused events stay in the graph as placeholders, so anyone can publish refused events to grow it. Needs per-author caps and rate limits. Priority: a Phase 2 gate.
- **Upstream bugs in p2panda-encryption.** The joiner membership overwrite in `dcgka.rs` `process_welcome`, seen with stock `TestDgm`. Missing `serde_bytes` on secret payloads. `receive` discards state on error (S3 c1). The orderer names the per-author latest and never prunes (S3 d). File upstream, with the patch at `spikes/shared/s3-history/logs/patch.diff`. Carry the patch until it lands.
- **artifact.rs signs serde_json.** Replace with cbor-core canonical bytes. There is no data to migrate (owner decision).
- **MSRV.** p2panda-core, -auth, -encryption, and -spaces need Rust 1.96. `pkgs/id` declares 1.91.0. Raise it before adopting. The toolchain pin (1.97.0) already satisfies it.
- **Two ed25519-dalek versions.** p2panda-core uses 2.2. Our code and iroh use 3.0. Keys cross as bytes, and both sides use `verify_strict` (S1, S5). Not a blocker. Revisit when adopting.
- **p2panda-store conflict.** sqlx-sqlite 0.9.0 needs libsqlite3-sys below 0.38, which rules out rusqlite 0.40 (S6). Another reason not to depend on p2panda-store.
- **Research note corrections** (appended to the research note). (a) The "unknown dependency panics" claim does not reproduce. An unknown dependency is an error. The real panic is "group absent at dependencies" (S2). (b) The yank range was overstated. Only the `.0` releases are yanked, and `=0.103.1` is not (S4). (c) LogSync's cost advantage holds only below about 35–40 chains (S1).
- **Observe ungated** (S4). See §3.7.

## 6. Decisions for the owner

Each item gives a recommendation and the default that applies without an answer.

1. **Dependents of voided events.** Authority-based, as p2panda does (recommended). Default: authority-based. Closing over all dependents changes outcomes (S2 3b).
2. **Relays and discovery.** Minimal, our own relays on home servers, and tickets (recommended). Default: this, once the relay exists. Until then, direct addresses only.
3. **Bare node-ID lookup.** Opt-in per command, with n0 named in the output (recommended). Default: off.
4. **`observe` gating.** Gate it, so existence and size stay private (recommended). Default: gate.
5. **Welcome growth.** Keep `full` as the default. File the `serde_bytes` fix upstream. Add a `recent:N` history option for groups above 1,000 devices, where full history becomes expensive. Default: `full`, with no cap, until S12 measures it at 1,000 rotations.
6. **In-group compromise.** Document remove and re-add as the only verified fix. Do not add an automatic periodic update, because it does not heal device compromise. Default: as stated.
7. **Content on removal.** Keep (recommended). Default: keep.
8. **Use-count bounds.** Drop them, because counts do not converge (recommended). Default: local enforcement until decided.
9. **Waiting and placeholder limits.** A per-author cap and an expiry. Proposed for tuning: 256 pending events per author and 7 days. Default: these values, before Phase 2 ends.
10. **Fork recovery.** Removal by managers, then re-enrolment under a new device key (recommended). Default: as stated.
11. **Upstream patch.** File the membership and `serde_bytes` fixes, and carry the patch until they merge (recommended). Default: as stated.
12. **Large-group scheme.** Gate end-to-end groups above 1,000 devices on a BeeKEM spike (S10 and a BeeKEM test) (recommended). Default: the gate applies.
13. **Transfer.** Keep it derived from the signed removal (S2) (recommended). Default: derived.

## 8. Plan with gates

Each phase ends with a gate. A gate passes when its tests run green on main.

- **Phase 0, now.** Fix the tuple prefix bug. Byte-cap pull pages. Raise the MSRV. Gate: S6's prefix cases pass on the fixed encoder.
- **Phase 1, events and storage.** Header with ChainExt, the canonical wrapper, the redb store, fork proofs, and bounded waiting. Gate: S1's header tests, S5's vectors and matrix, S6's durability and uniqueness cases, and S2's fork-freeze tests.
- **Phase 2, membership.** Auth-frontier shim, admission gate, placeholders, friendship pair rule and dissolve, and void reasons. Gate: the nine S2 scenarios as regression tests, each in every causal order.
- **Phase 3, sync, blobs, and transport.** Range sync on `/id-sync/1` over real stores. Blob fetch with EventMask gating and reset-code handling. Minimal endpoints, with our relay configured. Gate: S7 at 1,000 chains, and S4's gating tests on the real store.
- **Phase 4, delegation.** Edges, attenuation, chain search, the three modes, and expiry. World capabilities move to chain checks. Gate: S2's delegation cases, and the existing world tests.
- **Phase 5, encryption.** Our DGM, the heads orderer, the merge update at 32, from_join, scheme negotiation, `scheme: none`, the E2EE default, and warnings at 96, 128, and 1,000 by recipient device. Gate: S3's tests against our adapter, S10, and S12.
- **Phase 6, tags, documents, index, and search.** Gate: S8's property tests (observed-remove under any order), and S6's index tests on the real store.
- **Phase 7, invitations, then time and scale.** Seitan V2, gated on its test vector and the freshness check (S9). Witnesses after that. A large-group scheme, gated by §6 item 12.

## 10. Limits of this round

- **S1.** Range sync is a simulation in memory, not run over iroh. LogSync over iroh was not run.

- **S3.** Uses p2panda-encryption's test utilities and a patched copy. The production DGM does not exist yet. The heads orderer is untested.

- **S5.** 160,000 mutants, with no fuzzing campaign. dcbor interop checked on five vectors.
- **S6.** SIGKILL only, with no power-loss test. One machine.
- Everything ran on Linux with rustc 1.97.0 and p2panda main at `3e84cac`.
