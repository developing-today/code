## Decision summary

| Question | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| What a public share is | A **public grant**: an ordinary #5799 grant whose subject is the well-known **public principal** (everyone), issued by an explicit owner publish action and recorded in a **publication record**. No separate sharing mechanism (D1) |
| Discovery | Two modes per share, owner-chosen at publish time: **unlisted** (default — fetchable by anyone holding the share's public link, listed nowhere) and **listed** (also appears in the owner's public listing). There is **no global directory** of public shares in v1. The #5801 share manifest (minus key material) is adopted as the public share's provenance/integrity artifact and discovery payload (D2) |
| Limits | Public grants carry **`read` only**, plus `subscribe` if the owner enables updates; `write`/`append` are never public in v1. Classification ceiling ∩ grant applies unchanged — data classified above the public ceiling cannot be published at all. Serving-layer limits (per-share size and request-rate caps, per-owner aggregate caps) are mandatory, with the v1 defaults in D3; numeric values are configuration, not invariants (D3) |
| Public Turso databases | A public Turso database is a foreign collection set under a public grant, served **through the #5806 adapter's read path** — never by exposing the owner's Turso credential or a direct anonymous connection to the source. Publishing names the collections (tables) explicitly; tables created later are not auto-published (D4) |
| Unpublish | Unpublish **is** revocation of the public grant, with the parent's forward-only honesty boundary: new access stops at the next authorization check, discovery listings and manifest serving stop with it, hosted replicas are retracted via #5800 scope-exit, and the public identifier thereafter returns the same indistinguishable outcome as a nonexistent share. Copies already fetched cannot be recalled, and publish-time UX must say so. Re-publication mints a **new** share identifier; old links are never resurrected (D5) |
| Reserved | Whether public shares are also served to **fully anonymous (no-ID-identity) readers** via a public web gateway is Drewry's call (§6). Interim default, so implementation is not blocked: v1 serves ID peers (any identity, including a fresh pseudonymous one); the gateway is an additive later decision that changes no protocol rule in this record |

## 1. What the parent already fixed (not re-decided here)

From #5672 and its landed siblings, taken as given:

1. **The grants model** — per-object and per-collection grants; dimensions of who / what / operations / duration (standing, expiring, one-shot snapshot); delegation defaults to no and must be explicit, attenuated, and owner-visible. #5799 fixes the detail: operations are `read`, `subscribe` (requires `read`), `write`, `append`; durations are `standing`, `expires_at`, `snapshot_once`.
2. **Existence privacy** — a share must not leak the shape of what it does not include: no counts, IDs, or metadata of ungranted objects, and "no such object" is indistinguishable from "not granted" (#5799 invariant 6, #5800 §3). Publication is an owner's deliberate, scoped exception to this rule for the published slice only (D2); it is not a weakening of the rule anywhere else.
3. **The revocation honesty boundary** — revoking a grant stops future sync/access via ID; it cannot un-copy bytes a peer already holds in plaintext. D5 applies this boundary to unpublish verbatim and adds nothing to it.
4. **The adapter framing** — foreign collections are grantable resources under the same model; credentials are referenced, never stored in ID data or share payload; adapters are read/materialize-first in v1 (#5803). D4 applies this to public Turso databases.
5. **The transfer mechanics** — subset snapshot, per-grant scoped update stream, tombstones vs reason-free scope-exit markers, and receiving-side caps are #5800's, consumed here unchanged. A public share's snapshot and stream are exactly a #5800 snapshot and stream whose grant subject is the public principal.

## D1 — A public share is a public grant, created by a publish action

- The grant model gains one well-known subject: the **public principal**, meaning "any reader". A **public share** is a grant whose subject is the public principal. Everything else about it — scope (objects/collections), operations, duration — is an ordinary grant, evaluated by the same #5678/#5799 resolver at the same authorization points. There is no parallel "public" code path with its own authority rules; #5800 invariant 1 (every byte authorized by the grant at emission time) holds for public shares exactly as for private ones.
- Publication is a distinct, explicit **owner action**, never a side effect: setting a flag on a collection, sharing it privately, or serving it through an adapter never makes anything public. The publish action creates, atomically from the owner's point of view:
  1. the public grant;
  2. a **publication record** (owner-side object): share identifier, granted scope, operations, duration/expiry, discovery mode (D2), owner-supplied title/description, publish timestamp, and the classification ceiling the share was issued under.
- The **share identifier** is a fresh, unguessable identifier minted at publish time (not derived from object or collection ids), so the public link itself leaks nothing about the underlying store's shape, and re-publication can mint a new one (D5). The identifier is a locator, not a credential: the data behind it is public by the owner's choice, and D2 is explicit that unlisted mode is obscurity, not access control.
- A public grant **cannot be delegated** and cannot be re-granted by a reader. The parent's delegation machinery simply does not apply: there is no narrower subject than "everyone" to attenuate to, and a reader minting further shares of someone else's slice would break the one-grant-lineage rule #5801 D4 relies on for provenance. A reader who wants to re-share points at the owner's link.
- **Classification gate (from #5801 D5, applied):** effective shareability = classification ceiling ∩ grant scope. Data whose classification forbids public release cannot be published — the publish action refuses it, per object, at publish time and (for `subscribe` shares) at each update emission, exactly as #5800 re-evaluates scope per update. A collection that later receives an above-ceiling object does not leak it into the public stream; the object is simply never emitted, and per existence privacy its absence is not signalled.

## D2 — Discovery: unlisted by default, listed by choice, no global directory

Discovery answers "how does a reader find a public share?" with exactly three surfaces, and closes the rest:

1. **The link.** Every public share is fetchable by anyone who holds its public link/identifier, via the normal #5800 snapshot/stream flow against the public grant. This is the baseline: publication means link-fetchable.
2. **The owner's public listing (opt-in per share).** At publish time the owner chooses the share's discovery mode:
   - **Unlisted (the default):** the share appears in no listing, no index, and no sitemap-equivalent. Only the link discovers it.
   - **Listed:** the share additionally appears in the owner's public listing — the per-owner catalogue of that owner's listed public shares — with its publication record's title/description. Listing is per share, never per owner-global: publishing one listed share does not list the owner's other public shares, and the listing contains **only** listed public shares — never private grants, private share activity, or unlisted shares. The listing is itself served under the same rules as a public share of the publication records' public fields.
3. **The manifest as discovery payload.** Per #5801 §7's offer, this record **adopts** the share manifest for public shares, minus all key material (there is no restricted holder set for a key to protect): an owner-signed object carrying share id, grant lineage, granted-set commitment, epoch, expiry, and classification ceiling, extended here with the publication record's public fields (title, description, discovery mode, publish timestamp). Any client resolving a public link first receives the manifest, so provenance and integrity verification (#5801 D4 properties 1–4) work identically for public and encrypted shares, and a listing entry is just a manifest reference. A reader can therefore verify *completeness within the published set* and *provenance* — and, by the same deliberate symmetry as #5801, can prove nothing about anything the owner did not publish: there is no total-set commitment, and the manifest enumerates the published set only.

Explicitly **not** discovery surfaces in v1:

- **No global directory or firehose.** ID does not maintain a cross-owner index of public shares, and the protocol carries no "enumerate all public shares" operation. A global directory would make ID a publishing platform by default — with the spam, abuse-triage, and takedown obligations that implies — and no parent decision asks for one. Third parties may build indexes over *listed* shares out of band; that is their layer, not the protocol's.
- **No enumeration through the link space.** Share identifiers are unguessable (D1), and failed lookups return the indistinguishable-absence outcome (#5799 invariant 6): probing cannot distinguish "no such share" from "share exists but unlisted/unpublished", because unlisted shares answer link-holders identically whether or not any listing exists, and nonexistent identifiers answer exactly as unpublished ones do (D5).
- **Unlisted is not private.** Unlisted mode controls *discovery*, not *access*: anyone who obtains the link can read the share, and links leak (forwarding, screenshots, logs). UX copy at publish time must state this plainly — the honest label is "anyone with the link", never "private". (This is the public-share analogue of #5801's rule that copy must not overclaim take-back.)

## D3 — Limits: read-only, classified, capped

A public grant is the widest grant the model can express, so its limits are the strictest, and they are part of the grant/serving contract, not deployment afterthoughts:

1. **Operations: `read`, optionally `subscribe` — never `write` or `append`.** A public grant carrying a write operation is invalid at creation; the publish action cannot mint one. "Public write" (open submission/intake) is a different feature with its own abuse model and is out of scope here, not a variant of public sharing. Public Turso access inherits this: reads only (D4).
2. **Duration:** `standing`, `expires_at`, and `snapshot_once` are all permitted, with their #5799 meanings. A `snapshot_once` public share is a published static snapshot: it serves one cut, takes no updates, and unpublish (D5) is its only lifecycle event after publication.
3. **Classification ceiling** is recorded in the publication record and enforced per D1; it can only be at or below the owner's public-release ceiling. Encryption is not a public-share control (there is no holder set to protect); classification and scope are.
4. **Serving limits (mandatory, three layers).** Anonymous-class read load is borne by the owner's serving path and relays, so limits exist at:
   - **per-share:** maximum snapshot size (objects and bytes) the share will serve per reader, and a request-rate cap per share;
   - **per-owner aggregate:** a ceiling across all of an owner's public shares, so one viral share cannot starve the owner's other serving;
   - **receiving side:** #5800 §5 caps apply unchanged — any reader may cap, pause, or drop, and a relay hosting a public replica may enforce its own caps and drop the replica, reading to the owner as termination, not error.
   V1 starting defaults (configuration, tunable by the owner downward and by the platform as ceilings, **not** invariants): snapshot cap 100 MB or 10,000 objects per reader fetch, whichever is hit first; per-share rate cap 60 requests/minute per reader identity-or-address; owner aggregate defaults set by the serving deployment and reported to the owner. Hitting a cap returns the stable serving-limit outcome, never a partial silent truncation: a capped snapshot fetch says it was capped, and the reader may resume per #5800's chunk-resume rule.
5. **No ambient widening.** Limits and scope are evaluated from the public grant alone. A reader who also holds a private grant to overlapping data gets the union only through their own authenticated grants — the public path never borrows a private grant's scope, and the private path is unaffected by publication.

## D4 — Public Turso databases: through the adapter, never around it

"Public Turso database" means a Turso/libsql database (or a named subset of it) that its owner publishes. Under #5803/#5806 that database is a foreign store reached through the libsql adapter; publication therefore works as follows:

- The publishable unit is the **foreign collection** as #5803 defines it. Publishing a database = publishing an explicitly enumerated set of its collections (tables), listed in the publication record. It is a grant over those collections, not over the database handle: **tables created after publication are not published** until the owner amends the publication (an amendment is a new publish action against the same share only if scope is unchanged in kind — adding a collection mints an updated publication record and advances the manifest epoch; it never happens implicitly).
- **No credential ever becomes public.** The owner's Turso auth token stays a local credential reference on the owner's/serving side (#5803 rule, restated for the public case because it is the sharpest edge here): anonymous readers never receive, see, or connect with it. Public reads are served by the adapter's read path holding a **read-only** credential server-side, or from a **materialized public replica** the owner's side maintains — the owner's per-collection live/materialized choice under #5803, with #5803's rule that there is no automatic stale-snapshot fallback for a failed live read: if the live source is down, the public share is unavailable, and the outcome says so rather than silently serving old data as current. (A materialized replica is not a fallback; it is the declared mode, and its manifest states its cut/epoch.)
- **Queries are grant-scoped at the source.** Per #5803 §1 rule 2, the restriction is pushed into the source query: public readers can only run the adapter's read surface against the published collections — no arbitrary SQL against the database, no reaching unpublished tables through joins or views the adapter has not been configured to expose. If the adapter cannot enforce the restriction for a requested query shape, it refuses rather than fetching wide and filtering after the fact.
- **Unpublish for Turso** follows D5, plus: a materialized public replica is deleted on unpublish (it is the owner's own copy, so this half of take-back is complete, like #5801's sealed case); a live-served share simply stops authorizing.

## D5 — Unpublish: revocation, forward-only, links stay dead

- **Unpublish is grant revocation.** The owner revokes the public grant; there is no separate "hide" state. From the next authorization check (#5799: revocation stops future replication at the next authorization check):
  1. snapshot and update serving stop — no new reader can fetch, no subscribed reader receives further updates;
  2. the share leaves the owner's public listing (if listed) and its manifest stops being served;
  3. relays/peers hosting the public replica receive the #5800 **scope-exit** signal for the whole share and drop the replica — scope-exit is reason-free, as #5800 specifies, so the retraction itself does not narrate why;
  4. the public identifier resolves to the **indistinguishable-absence** outcome: after unpublish, "this share was unpublished", "this share never existed", and "no such share" are the same protocol-visible result. Existence privacy re-attaches in full at unpublish.
- **Bounded staleness, stated honestly.** Serving caches and third-party indexes are not the protocol: caches in ID's own serving path must honour revocation within their cache lifetime, and the public-share cache lifetime is capped at **5 minutes** (configuration ceiling, not a target). Copies outside ID's serving path — a reader's downloaded snapshot, a search engine's cache, an archive — are outside the promise entirely (below).
- **The honesty boundary, verbatim from the parent:** unpublish cannot recall bytes any reader already fetched. Public data is plaintext in readers' hands by design; forward-only is the whole promise, and it is stronger here only in the two places the owner controls the copies — hosted replicas (retracted, above) and materialized Turso replicas (deleted, D4). Publish-time UX must state the boundary before the owner confirms publication: "Unpublishing stops new access through ID. It cannot take back copies anyone already made." Any copy claiming stronger take-back is wrong, per #5801 invariant 4's rule for share claims generally.
- **Re-publication mints a new share.** Publishing the same slice again creates a new public grant, a new share identifier, and a new publication record. The old identifier is **never resurrected and never redirects**: stale links stay dead rather than silently reviving under a link the owner may have distributed in a context they no longer endorse. The owner's own listing links only the live share.
- **Unpublish is not delete, and delete still propagates.** Unpublishing changes access, not the underlying data: the objects, the collection, and any private grants over them are untouched. Conversely, while a share is live, deleting a published object propagates to public readers as a #5800 tombstone through the update stream, exactly as for private subscribers.
- **Owner-side accounting survives unpublish.** The publication record is retained in the owner's private audit surface after unpublish — what slice was public, under what mode, from when to when — because the owner cannot otherwise account for a past exposure. The retained record is owner-only, is never itself discoverable (D2), and its retention is the owner's choice to purge. This is the one place the system remembers a share existed after unpublish, and it is deliberately on the private side of the line.

## 5. Invariants implementation must hold

1. **One authority path.** Every byte served under a public share is authorized by the public grant, evaluated by the #5678/#5799 resolver at emission time. No public-specific bypass, cache, or adapter shortcut may serve data the resolver would refuse.
2. **Publication is explicit and atomic.** Nothing becomes public except by the publish action; the grant, publication record, and manifest come into existence together, and unpublish revokes them together.
3. **Existence privacy outside the slice.** Public surfaces (link resolution, listings, manifests, error outcomes) reveal nothing about unpublished objects, unlisted shares, private grants, or — after unpublish — the share itself. No totals, no counts, no "N of M" anywhere on a public surface (#5800 §3).
4. **Read-only public.** No public grant with `write` or `append` can exist; the type/creation path rejects it, not just the UI.
5. **Credentials never cross.** No credential, token, or secret-bearing locator appears in any public artifact — manifest, listing, snapshot, error text, or log line — at any level (#5803; the #5801 invariant 5 logging rule applied to adapter credentials).
6. **Forward-only take-back is the stated promise** — in code comments, UX copy, and docs. Nothing may claim that unpublish recalls fetched copies.
7. **Dead links stay dead.** An unpublished share identifier is never reused, resurrected, or redirected; a new publication is a new identifier.
8. **Caps fail loudly.** Serving-limit refusals are stable, named outcomes; no public fetch silently truncates, silently serves stale-as-current, or silently widens scope to satisfy a request.

## 6. Reserved for Drewry (one item, with interim default)

**Anonymous-web access.** This record settles the protocol for readers who arrive as ID peers — including a reader who spins up a fresh pseudonymous identity, which is a low bar by design. Whether public shares are *also* exposed to fully anonymous readers with no ID identity at all, through a public web gateway (plain HTTPS/browser access to a share link), is a product and platform commitment — public hosting, abuse triage, and takedown handling — rather than a protocol question, and it is **Drewry's call**. Interim default so nothing blocks on it: **v1 is ID-peer access only**; if the gateway is later approved, it is an additional serving front-end over exactly these grants, manifests, limits, and unpublish rules, and changes no decision above. Everything else this issue names — discovery, limits, unpublish semantics — is decided in D1–D5 without depending on the answer.

## 7. Alternatives rejected (summary)

- **A separate public-sharing mechanism** (its own ACLs, its own serving path) — forks the authority model, guarantees the two paths drift, and voids #5800 invariant 1. The public principal inside the existing grants model gets every parent guarantee for free (D1).
- **Global directory / firehose of all public shares** — turns ID into a publishing platform with attendant abuse obligations; no parent decision asks for it; listed-mode per-owner catalogues cover the legitimate discovery need (D2).
- **Listed by default** — publication already widens access to everyone; making catalogue-listing the default widens *discovery* beyond the owner's explicit intent. Unlisted-by-default keeps the two widenings separately consented (D2).
- **Public write/append** ("open submission shares") — a different feature (intake) with an unsolved abuse model; letting it ride on the read-sharing design would import write abuse into a read contract (D3).
- **Serving public Turso by handing out a scoped token or direct connection string** — a credential in readers' hands is a credential out of the owner's control: it survives unpublish, escapes grant scoping, and violates #5803's referenced-never-stored rule in spirit and letter (D4).
- **Whole-database publication with auto-included future tables** — scope would grow without a publish action, violating D1's explicitness and #5799's scope discipline (D4).
- **Unpublish as "hide"** (data still served to link-holders, just delisted) — a state that looks revoked and is not is worse than either honest state; it would make D5's promises unverifiable (D5).
- **Resurrecting or redirecting old links on re-publication** — silently revives distribution contexts the owner may no longer endorse; a new identifier costs the owner one new link and keeps the semantics auditable (D5).
- **Promising full recall on unpublish** (including reader-held copies) — false for plaintext by the parent's own boundary; the forward-only promise is the strongest true statement (#5672, #5801 D3).

## 8. Divergence check against the parents

One deliberate scoping, recorded explicitly per this issue's instruction — **not** a change to a parent decision: existence privacy (#5672 / #5799 invariant 6) says a peer must not be able to distinguish "no such object" from "not granted". For a *published* slice, the owner deliberately suspends that protection for the slice itself — that is what publication means — while D2/D5 keep the rule fully in force for everything outside the slice and re-attach it to the slice itself at unpublish. The parent's rule governs shares a peer has no right to; a public share is the owner's recorded decision that everyone has the right. No other parent decision is changed: the grants model, the revocation boundary, the adapter contract, and the manifest design are consumed as fixed, and D4/D5's Turso and replica rules are applications of #5803/#5801, not amendments to them. If a future design needs publication to mean something stronger (e.g. a public grant that survives owner revocation, or guaranteed archival), that is a change to this record and to the parent's revocation boundary, and must be filed as such.

## What this record does not do

No code, no wire format beyond the manifest field vocabulary inherited from #5801, and no changes to the grants resolver, the #5800 protocol, or the libsql adapter are specified. Implementation work (public principal in the grants model, publish/unpublish actions and publication record, owner listing surface, serving-limit enforcement, adapter public-read path) is follow-on work to be filed from this record; #5802 stays open until this record is merged, per its acceptance.
