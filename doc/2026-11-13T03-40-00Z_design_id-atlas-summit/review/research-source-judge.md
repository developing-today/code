# Research source-fidelity judge — #9106

**Review date:** 2026-11-12. **Scope:** source fidelity and evidence only across `crosscut/research.md`, `part-a-addressing/research.md`, `part-a-graph/research.md`, `part-b/research.md`, `part-c/research.md`, and `baseline/v1-audit.md`. I did not assess or reopen design choices. External pages below were checked read-only on the review date; current hosted-service figures are time-sensitive.

## Verdict

**Mostly agree with the evidence framing; not yet evidence-clean.** The notes usually separate observed source facts, bounded repository evidence, and recommendations. The V1 audit is unusually careful about implementation versus deployment status, and its producer-specific hash distinction is supported by the pinned upstream implementation. No production performance winner is asserted. Two blocking corrections are needed: Part B overstates what RFC 9750 says about replay, and Part A Graph's experiment report contradicts its checked-in runnable script about SQLite JSONB. Two other source attributions need tighter wording (RFC 9942 and OCapN attenuation).

## Blockers / smallest required corrections

1. **Part B §“Replay, rotation and revocation” (line 17): RFC 9750 is characterized too broadly.** The cited RFC is the April 2025 _MLS Architecture_ (Informational), and §8.6 is explicitly titled “No Protection Against Replay by Insiders.” That is narrower than “replay within an epoch remains an application concern”; the latter reads as a general MLS replay property. Keep the proposed stable event ID/counter as this design's recommendation, but say RFC 9750 warns that MLS does not prevent replay by malicious insiders (and cite §8.6). Do not attribute the particular event-ID/counter solution to the RFC. [RFC 9750](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9750.html), §8.6; page metadata states April 2025 and Informational.

2. **Part A Graph §micro-experiment (lines 36–51): written result is stale/inconsistent with the executable.** I ran the checked-in `graph-encoding-toy.py` with `python3 -I`. The script emits `sqlite_jsonb_typed_octet_arrays: bytes=115656 sha256=1cba765a5f74c2f6554b9adbac1a34406beeb8e854c92ec0b251cd46f2e10290`, reports `sqlite_version=3.53.3`, checks JSONB round-trip, and separately checks arbitrary non-UTF-8 bytes. But the research text says “no SQLite JSONB bytes were generated” and its reported round-trip list omits both JSONB and the arbitrary-byte probe. Either update the method/results and explicitly label this one SQLite build's opaque JSONB output as a local format-specific observation (not portable or a ranking), or remove that code path from the claimed experiment. Keep the result firmly in toy-serialization scope. SQLite itself says JSONB is intended for internal SQLite use, is not PostgreSQL-binary-compatible, and most operations remain O(N): [SQLite JSON1/JSONB](https://www.sqlite.org/json1.html), §§3.2–3.2.1.

## Required evidence wording / attribution

3. **Part B line 19: qualify “a named party made a receipt/commitment.”** RFC 9942 says COSE Receipts are signed proofs of properties of a Verifiable Data Structure, true when issued; its concrete profile covers RFC 9162 SHA-256 Merkle inclusion and consistency. A valid COSE signature authenticates a key, while mapping that key to a named party is an identity-binding step outside the receipt format. Minimal correction: say the receipt can evidence a signed VDS property/statement at issuance time; a named-party attribution additionally depends on validating the signer-key binding. The surrounding limit that a receipt does not prove continuing retention/availability is sound. [RFC 9942](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9942.html), §§1, 5.2–5.3; published June 2026 as Standards Track.

4. **Part B lines 12, 24, 30: capability references are evidenced; attenuation is not established by the cited OCapN sources.** The retrieved OCapN CapTP draft describes authority as “if and only if” an object possesses a reference and describes third-party handoff as sharing/redeeming a reference. Its implementation guide calls CapTP capability-secure, and the repository labels the specification a draft likely to change significantly. Those support capability-based references, handoff, and transport separation; they do not, in the cited passages, establish general attenuable references. Retain attenuation as a design possibility/research question or add a precise primary-source section and clearly label its draft status. Do not present it as a verified OCapN feature. [CapTP draft](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn/blob/main/draft-specifications/CapTP%20Specification.md), Introduction and “Third Party Handoffs”; [implementation guide](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn/blob/main/implementation-guide/Implementation%20Guide.md); [Netlayers draft](https://github.com/ocapn/ocapn/blob/main/draft-specifications/Netlayers.md).

## Verified claims and acceptable inferences

- **Diamond Types / Eg-walker and intent:** Part B line 10 is appropriately careful: the authors' page identifies an optimized Eg-walker text CRDT, gives the March 2025 EuroSys context, and reports text-workload performance; it does not establish Drew's intended referent. The text explicitly calls that identification an assumption, not a fact. [Authors' page](https://martin.kleppmann.com/2025/03/30/eg-walker-collaborative-text.html).
- **Willow status and dates:** The cited Willow pages do mark Willow’25 as **Proposal (as of 05.17.2025)** and Confidential Sync as **Proposal (as of 21.11.2025)**; the Confidential Sync page says work is paused and much of that design is outdated. The report correctly avoids treating it as a settled drop-in protocol. Willow’25 directly specifies Ed25519 and Meadowcap parameters. [Willow’25](https://willowprotocol.org/specs/willow25/); [Confidential Sync](https://willowprotocol.org/specs/confidential-sync/).
- **Snowflake model vs Cortex:** Part C line 31 correctly separates the current selected `arctic-embed-m-v2.0` model from Snowflake Cortex as a vendor/service family. Its model choice is attributed to the dated internal #9092 snapshot, not inferred from Cortex docs. The statement that the second embedder is undecided is likewise explicitly attributed to that snapshot. This internal source was not independently re-audited here.
- **Cloud limits/currentness:** Current Cloudflare docs checked on 2026-11-12 say D1's paid per-database cap is 10 GB and list rows-read/written as billing metrics; the D1 limits page was last updated Oct 30, 2026. Current Durable Objects docs say SQLite-backed storage is 10 GB/object, alarm wall time 15 minutes, and individual-object throughput is a _soft_ 1,000 requests/s; the page was last updated Nov 7, 2026. Part C correctly distinguishes these as service limits, not measured performance. Keep account/plan qualifiers and page dates with any copied figures. [D1 limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/limits/); [D1 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/); [Durable Objects limits](https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/).
- **Turso transaction semantics:** The current Turso TypeScript reference describes batch statements as sequential in an implicit transaction, with all changes committed on success and rolled back on failure; it separately describes interactive transaction modes and warns that libSQL interactive writes can hold a lock with a five-second timeout. The research's insistence on version/package-specific verification and on not inferring SLA/durability from transaction syntax is appropriate. Avoid generalizing behavior across `@tursodatabase/*` and `@libsql/client` without naming the package/runtime. [Turso TypeScript reference](https://docs.turso.tech/sdk/ts/reference).
- **Pinned ID hash profile:** `pkgs/id/Cargo.lock` pins `iroh-blobs` 0.103.1. The corresponding tagged upstream source says `Hash::new` calls `blake3::hash` on the provided bytes, exposes a 32-byte array, and renders `to_hex()`. This supports the baseline's bounded claim about the Iroh blob hash profile; it does not collapse event/artifact/world commitments into that profile. [Pinned upstream source](https://github.com/n0-computer/iroh-blobs/blob/v0.103.1/src/hash.rs).
- **JSONB performance wording:** The official SQLite documentation supports “smaller/faster than text” and O(N) for most operations, while explicitly distinguishing its private format from PostgreSQL JSONB. Part A Graph correctly says this does not establish Turso/libSQL parity and treats the proposed backend use as inference, not a measured graph-index result.
- **Baseline boundary:** The audit itself says source-only at a fixed checkout SHA and explicitly does not establish deployment/runtime status. Its distinctions among ID blob store, event store, world journal, and Atlas `content_hash` consumer are appropriately bounded. The upstream hash source was spot-checked as above; I did not independently re-run the full repository audit or verify deployment claims.

## Evidence boundary

This was a targeted source-fidelity check, not a line-by-line retrieval of every URL in all five research ledgers. Findings above identify the concrete mismatches/qualifications found and the primary sources checked. Recommendations (e.g. stable IDs/counters, capability ports, conformance matrices, no global ordering) remain acceptable as design inferences when labeled as such; none should be described as an externally verified product fact or production winner.
