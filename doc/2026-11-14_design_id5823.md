## 1. Decision in one paragraph

The currency backend port is the PoC seam in `pkgs/id/src/currency_poc/`
**formalised and generalised**: the `LedgerBackend` trait shape proven by the
conversion-executor PoC (#2289) — batched account creation, batched transfers
with linked-chain atomicity, per-item typed results, caller-generated
write-ahead IDs, and fallible lookups — becomes the port every currency stage
implements, extended with the three things the PoC never needed: a pending
(hold) transfer lifecycle, balances that include pending amounts, and an
ordered per-account history surface. A backend is called a **currency
backend** if and only if it passes the invariant test-suite contract in §4,
run **unmodified** against it through the port alone: one suite, one pass
rule, no per-stage waivers. The suite's invariants are I1–I8 as operationally
restated by #5829 §3.4 — this record adopts those identifiers as the binding
vocabulary (the authority #5824 §7 reserved to this record) and supplies the
concrete conformance cases, the pass rule, and the evidence form underneath
them. No parent #5676 decision is changed (§7); the one strengthening —
holds are a required port capability, not an optional one — is recorded
explicitly in §7 rather than folded in silently.

## 2. Where the port sits

- The port is at the **ledger level**: accounts, transfers, balances,
  history. It is not the user-facing currency API. Parent principle 1 (no
  backend concept ever leaks into the user-facing API) and principle 4
  (swap, don't rewrite) both fix this boundary: the user-facing transfer
  API from #5676's interface list (create transfer with idempotency key,
  query balance, query history, hold/capture/release) lives **above** the
  port and is identical at every stage; the port is what stage adapters
  implement **below** it.
- The shared layers above the port — admission, the write-ahead intent
  discipline, conversion composition (#2063/#5828), reconciliation
  reporting — are written once against the port and do not know which
  stage is underneath. (This is the system-under-test shape #5829 §2
  already assumes.)
- Stage adapters: Stage A is the journal+projection composite of #5824
  (its commit point is the journal append; Redis is never the authority);
  Stage B is a per-shard adapter set plus a coordinator presenting one
  port face (#5826 owns the sharding mechanics); Stage C is a TigerBeetle
  binding. An adapter presents **one** port face regardless of how many
  stores it coordinates internally.

## 3. The port

### 3.1 Data model (formalised from the PoC)

- A **currency is a `ledger: u32`** (non-zero); a transfer's ledger must
  equal both accounts' ledgers. Cross-ledger transfers are forbidden at
  the port, permanently: conversions are composed pairs of same-ledger
  transfers (per the #2040 investigation, carried by the PoC and by
  #5828), never a port primitive.
- **Amounts are `u128`** in the currency's smallest unit; zero-amount
  transfers are rejected (`AmountMustNotBeZero`).
- **Accounts**: caller-chosen `u128` ID (zero and int-max reserved and
  rejected), `ledger`, application chart-of-accounts `code: u16`, and
  balance-rule flags (`debits_must_not_exceed_credits`,
  `credits_must_not_exceed_debits`). Accounts are created with zero
  balances, in batches, with per-item results.
- **Transfers**: caller-generated `u128` ID, debit account, credit
  account (must differ), amount, `ledger`, `code`, correlation stamp
  `user_data_128`, and the `linked` chain flag (§3.2). IDs are generated
  and **persisted write-ahead by the caller before submission** — the
  PoC's intent discipline (`currency_poc/intent.rs`, `recovery.rs`) is
  part of the port contract, not an executor habit: recovery from any
  crash window is defined as lookup of pre-generated IDs (§4, cases
  R1–R2), so an adapter must make a submitted transfer findable by ID
  and must never mint IDs on the caller's behalf.
- **Balances** report four figures per account — debits pending, debits
  posted, credits pending, credits posted. This extends the PoC's
  posted-only `AccountBalance`, which was sufficient for a conversion
  executor and is not sufficient once holds are port-level (§3.3);
  `spendable` is derived from these four under the account's flags,
  exactly as the PoC derived it from the posted pair.
- Every committed posting is stamped with its correlation value and is
  retrievable by it (`try_transfers_with_user_data_128` in the PoC):
  the conversion lookup the acceptance criteria of #2063 asked for is a
  port obligation.

### 3.2 Operations

The port is one trait-shaped surface (Rust-first, as the codebase is;
the contract is the semantics, not the syntax). Operation names below
are the PoC's, retained deliberately — #5824 §7 binds naming conflicts
in this record's favour, and keeping the proven names costs nothing.

| Operation                                                                                       | Contract                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `create_accounts(batch)`                                                                        | One result per submitted account, in submission order. Re-creating an identical account returns `Exists` (idempotent retry, treated as success); same ID with different fields returns `ExistsWithDifferentFields` and changes nothing.                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `create_transfers(batch)`                                                                       | One result per submitted transfer, in submission order. Contiguous runs ending at a transfer with `linked == false` form **linked chains that post atomically or not at all**. A batch ending in an unterminated chain rejects that chain atomically (final leg `LinkedEventChainOpen`, earlier legs `LinkedEventFailed`, nothing posts, legs may be resubmitted properly terminated); terminated chains earlier in the same batch are unaffected. |
| Pending lifecycle: `create_transfers` with pending flag, `post_pending(id)`, `void_pending(id)` | §3.3.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `try_lookup_transfer(id)`                                                                       | Fallible: `Ok(Some)` / `Ok(None)` / `Err`. Absence and backend failure are different answers and must never be conflated.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `try_lookup_account(id)`                                                                        | Same fallible shape.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| `try_account_balance(id)`                                                                       | Same fallible shape; returns the four-figure balance of §3.1.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `try_transfers_with_user_data_128(value)`                                                       | All **posted** transfers carrying the correlation stamp. (Pending transfers are found by ID, not by correlation — the PoC's posted-only semantics, retained.)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| `try_account_history(id, after_sequence, limit)`                                                | The account's postings in commit order (§3.4). This is the one operation with no PoC counterpart; it exists because invariant I5 (auditability) must be checkable through the port alone.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

Rules binding every operation:

- **Result-length rule (#4669):** a batch call returns exactly one
  result per submitted item. Any other length is a backend failure,
  never a success, and callers must treat it as such.
- **Exists-in-chain rule (#6170):** a linked chain that mixes an
  idempotent `Exists` leg with new legs posts **nothing** — the `Exists`
  leg reports `Exists`, every other leg `LinkedEventFailed` — because
  the underlying semantics (TigerBeetle's) break a chain on any
  non-created status. The single exception is a chain whose legs are
  **all** identical resubmissions: per-leg `Exists`, nothing moves. A
  lone unlinked transfer is a chain of one and keeps plain `Exists`
  semantics. Adapters whose native store does not behave this way must
  emulate it; this rule is where "same semantics, different store" is
  easiest to get wrong, so the suite tests it directly (§4, A3–A4).
- **Failed-ID rule:** a transfer ID that has failed is remembered by
  the backend; resubmitting it returns `IdAlreadyFailed`, never the
  original (now possibly stale) error. Recovery flows depend on this
  distinction (#6628).
- **Error vocabulary:** per-item results use a closed, typed error
  vocabulary (the PoC's `AccountError` / `TransferError` sets are the
  seed). An adapter maps native errors onto it exhaustively; an
  unmapped native status surfaces as a typed `UnmodelledStatus` /
  `BackendFailure`, never as success and never as a panic. The
  vocabulary changes only by amendment to this record.
- **Lookups on critical paths are the `try_` forms.** The PoC's
  best-effort wrappers (log-and-default) are conveniences for tests
  and inspection; they are **not** part of the port contract, and no
  production path may be built on a lookup that cannot distinguish
  "absent" from "backend down".
- **Signature shape:** batch operations take `&mut self` in the PoC.
  The port does not mandate a concurrency model; it mandates that
  whatever serialisation an adapter needs is the adapter's own
  business and invisible above the port. An adapter's conformance
  statement (§4.4) declares its concurrency claim.

### 3.3 Pending transfers (holds) are part of the port

The parent interface list carries "hold/capture/release **if
supported**", and #5676's Stage C design brief names TigerBeetle's
pending/post/void two-phase transfers. A port in which holds are
optional cannot carry invariant I6 (hold integrity), and Stage B/C
designs would each invent their own hold semantics above the port —
the fork this record exists to prevent. Decided:

- The port includes a **pending lifecycle**: a transfer may be created
  **pending** (reserving funds against the debit account's balance
  rule without posting), then exactly one of **posted** (via
  `post_pending`) or **voided** (via `void_pending`) happens; a pending
  transfer may carry a timeout after which it voids itself. Posting or
  voiding twice, posting a voided transfer, and voiding a posted one
  are typed errors, and repeating the _same_ terminal action with the
  same ID is an idempotent `Exists`-class outcome, per the suite (§4,
  H2).
- Pending amounts appear in the four-figure balance (§3.1) and are
  never spendable by a second transfer: the balance rule evaluates
  posted + pending debits together (I2/I6).
- How an adapter implements pending is its own design: TigerBeetle
  natively; Stage A as journaled state in the regular store with the
  projection reflecting reservations (#5824's journal-first ordering
  is unchanged — a hold is a journal entry like any other); Stage B
  per its saga record. The port fixes the **semantics**; §7 records
  the "if supported" strengthening explicitly.

### 3.4 History, events, and ordering

- Every posting (including pending creation, post, and void) is an
  entry in the adapter's history for both affected accounts, in
  **commit order**, carrying a per-adapter monotonic sequence.
  `try_account_history` pages through it deterministically.
- The events other ID subsystems consume (#5676's interface list, I8)
  are emitted from this same history: one event per posting, in
  history order per account. An adapter may not emit an event for a
  posting its history does not contain, nor contain a posting it
  never emits an event for. Event _transport_ is above the port;
  event _fidelity_ is a port obligation.
- Balance reads: the port does not fix a consistency level for reads
  above it — #5824 already ruled for Stage A (read-your-committed-
  writes for posting decisions; stated-staleness reads only for
  explicitly approximate consumers), and the equivalent ruling per
  stage belongs to the stage records. The port fixes only that the
  four-figure balance an adapter returns is a balance the adapter can
  defend from its history (I5).

### 3.5 Relation to the PoC — formalised, then superseded as the standard

- **Formalised (kept, now contractual):** the trait shape and operation
  names; the data model of §3.1 as far as the PoC took it; linked-chain
  atomicity including the unterminated-chain and Exists-in-chain rules;
  the result-length rule; the failed-ID rule; write-ahead caller IDs;
  the intent/recovery pattern as the defined crash semantics.
- **Extended (new in this record):** pending lifecycle (§3.3);
  four-figure balances; per-account sequenced history (§3.4); the
  closed error vocabulary as a versioned contract; the conformance
  gate itself (§4).
- **Superseded:** from this record on, the standard is **this record
  plus the suite**, not the PoC code. `currency_poc` remains a PoC
  under the #2056 programme rule; its two implementations
  (`InMemoryLedger`, and `TigerBeetleLedger` in `tb_ledger.rs` behind
  `currency-poc-tb`) are the first two candidate adapters and its
  scenario tests (`currency_poc/tests.rs`, the #2063 §4 cases) are the
  suite's seed corpus (§4.5) — evidence and starting material, not the
  definition. When implementation is scheduled, the port moves out of
  `currency_poc` into its production home as a follow-on (§8); nothing
  production-shaped is built on the PoC module itself.

### 3.6 Stage-declared semantics

The port is identical at every stage; two things are **declared per
stage**, in the adapter's conformance statement, before the suite runs:

- **Outcome vocabulary for multi-leg operations.** Stage A and C claim
  the atomic outcomes of #5828 (`POSTED` / `FAILED_ATOMIC`); Stage B
  additionally declares its pending-settlement saga outcomes for
  cross-shard operations. Invariant I3 is "atomicity **as declared**":
  the suite judges a stage against its declared outcomes, never
  against atomicity it does not claim (#5829 §3.2, same rule for its
  oracle).
- **Balance-read consistency** actually provided (§3.4).

Nothing else may vary. A difference not declared here is a
non-conformance, not a stage personality.

## 4. The invariant test-suite contract

### 4.1 Form

- The suite is **one executable suite** that drives a backend **only
  through the port** (§3). It knows no adapter internals, no native
  client, no store-specific query language.
- The adapter under test supplies exactly one thing: a **factory**
  that produces a fresh, empty backend per case (or per case class,
  where a case says so). Setup beyond the factory — accounts,
  currencies, funds — happens through the port, in the open.
- The suite is the **conformance gate**: within the #5829 verification
  programme it runs unmodified as **leg 0**, before that programme's
  generative, crash-injection, and load legs. Those legs are the
  programme's; this contract owns the gate they build on, and does not
  restate them.
- The **in-memory reference backend is the control**: it must pass
  every case trivially. A case that fails against the control has a
  broken case (or a broken oracle assumption), not a broken adapter,
  and is fixed by suite amendment (§4.5) before it gates anyone
  (#5829 §2, adopted).

### 4.2 The invariants

The suite exists to prove #5676 principle 3 — double-entry invariants
are backend-independent — in the operational form #5829 §3.4 fixed:

- **I1 Conservation:** per currency, the sum of all balances changes
  only by explicit issuance/retirement — never by a transfer,
  conversion, crash, or retry.
- **I2 Balance rule:** no account goes negative beyond its currency's
  stated rules (the port form: the account's balance-rule flags hold
  against posted + pending debits/credits at every observable point).
- **I3 Atomicity as declared:** an operation reaches exactly one of
  its stage's declared outcomes; no partial legs are observable
  outside declared pending states.
- **I4 Idempotency:** re-submitting a committed idempotency key (port
  form: a committed transfer/account ID with identical fields) is a
  no-op returning the original outcome, indefinitely.
- **I5 Auditability:** the observable history replays to the current
  balances — the ledger is its own proof.
- **I6 Hold integrity:** held funds are neither spendable twice nor
  stranded: every hold eventually posts, voids, or expires by its
  stated rule, under crashes and retries alike.
- **I7 Conversion integrity:** at the port level — a conversion's legs
  are findable by their correlation stamp, agree in amount/ledger per
  leg, and post all-or-nothing as one linked chain. (End-to-end
  conversion semantics — rate authorisation records, cap reservation —
  live above the port and are #5828's and the programme's to test; the
  port-level claim is the composition primitive they rest on.)
- **I8 Event fidelity:** emitted events match postings one-for-one, in
  order per account (§3.4).

### 4.4 Pass rule and conformance evidence

- **Pass = every case in the current catalogue, run unmodified,
  passes.** No waivers, no adapter-specific case edits, no
  "passes except". A case may carry a **stage-conditional
  expectation** only where §3.6 declares the semantics differ (in
  practice: I3 outcome vocabulary for Stage B cross-shard
  operations), and the conditional expectation must be written in
  the adapter's conformance statement **before** the run — never
  negotiated after a failure.
- An adapter is called a currency backend when it has a **current
  conformance statement**: adapter build identifier, suite version
  (this record's date + catalogue version), the §3.6 declarations,
  per-case results, and the environment the run executed in. The
  statement is published with the adapter (the #5829 programme
  consumes these as leg-0 evidence).
- A failure is a finding against the adapter — or, if it reproduces
  against the in-memory control, against the suite (§4.1). Either
  way it is recorded with its minimal repro; a red gate is a
  publishable result, not a private embarrassment (the programme's
  oracle-first rule, #5829 decision 2, applies to the gate too).

### 4.5 Suite evolution

- Cases are **added or strengthened** by amendment to this record
  (or its successor record); a case is never silently weakened or
  deleted — a wrong case is retired by an amendment that says why,
  and the retired ID is never reused.
- The seed corpus is the PoC's scenario suite: the #2063 §4 cases
  already encoded in `currency_poc/tests.rs` (posted conversion
  findable by `user_data_128`; atomic failure moving nothing; both
  crash windows without double-posting; LP pre-flight rejection;
  the write-ahead intent cases) are lifted into the catalogue's
  form when the suite is implemented — several catalogue cases
  above (R1, R2, R4, TC3) are those scenarios generalised from
  conversions to the port as a whole.
- Every failure found by the #5829 programme's generative leg that
  shrinks to a deterministic port-level repro is **promoted** into
  this catalogue by amendment — the gate grows from evidence, the
  same regression-corpus discipline #5829 §3.5 sets for the
  programme.

## 5. Invariants implementation must hold

For whoever implements the port, an adapter, or the suite — the
checklist form of §§3–4:

1. Balanced postings only: every posted chain's legs sum to zero per
   currency; a chain posts completely or not at all (subject only to
   §3.6 declared outcomes).
2. Result vectors are exactly one-per-item, in order; anything else
   is a backend failure (#4669).
3. `Exists` means identical-and-already-there; different fields under
   a taken ID are an error, never an overwrite — for accounts,
   transfers, chains (A1–A3), and pending terminal actions alike.
4. Failed transfer IDs are remembered and refuse resubmission as
   `IdAlreadyFailed`.
5. IDs are caller-generated and write-ahead; reserved IDs (zero,
   int-max) are rejected with typed errors ahead of any existence
   lookup.
6. Critical-path lookups distinguish absent from unavailable
   (`try_` forms); no production path uses a defaulting lookup.
7. Holds reserve against posted + pending together, resolve exactly
   once (post, void, or timeout), and never strand funds (I6).
8. History is complete, sequenced, and sufficient to reconstruct
   every balance (I5); events mirror history exactly (I8).
9. Cross-ledger movement never appears as a single transfer;
   conversions compose same-ledger legs under one correlation stamp
   (I7).
10. No backend concept (Redis keys, Turso shards, TigerBeetle wire
    types) appears in the port's types or errors as seen from above
    the port — the error vocabulary and this data model are the whole
    surface.
11. RateAuthorisation (#2290) and conversion composition (#2063)
    stay above the port: the port records the authorisation
    reference a transfer carries (in its metadata/correlation
    surface) but never decides authorisation itself (#5828 boundary,
    unchanged).

## 6. Alternatives rejected

- **The user-facing transfer API as the port.** Rejected: it would
  bake today's service shape into every backend and leak stage
  differences upward — the exact failure parent principles 1 and 4
  exist to prevent. The API's stability is the _goal_; the port is
  the mechanism, one level down.
- **A generic storage/repository port** (get/put rows, adapters
  interpret). Rejected: every invariant in §4.2 would move above the
  port and be re-implemented per stage — conservation, chain
  atomicity, and idempotency are properties of the ledger operations
  themselves, and a CRUD seam cannot state them, let alone let one
  suite prove them once.
- **Per-stage ports with a translation layer.** Rejected: the suite
  would fork per stage (the same reasoning by which #5829 rejected
  per-stage suites), "backend-independent invariants" would become a
  claim about three different contracts, and the translation layer
  becomes a fourth, unowned backend.
- **Promoting `currency_poc` to the production port as-is.**
  Rejected: it is conversion-shaped (no holds, no history, posted-only
  balances), it is explicitly a PoC under the #2056 programme rule
  ("nothing production-shaped may be built on top of it without a
  review round" — `currency_poc/mod.rs`), and until this record there
  was no conformance gate for it to pass. This record _is_ the review
  round; §3.5 states what survives it.
- **TigerBeetle's wire protocol as the literal port** (adapters speak
  to a TB-shaped service API, error codes and all). Rejected as
  stated — the port adopts TigerBeetle's _semantics_ where they are
  the proven ones (chains, flags, pending lifecycle) but its types

- **The suite as a documentation checklist** (conformance by review
