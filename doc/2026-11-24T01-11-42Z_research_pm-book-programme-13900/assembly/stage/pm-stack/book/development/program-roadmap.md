# PM stack research and implementation roadmap

The PM stack's design record is three documents written on 2026-11-11 and 2026-11-12, plus the store plans that follow from them. The design register (`pm-stack/design-register-2026-11-11.md`) records 117 designs — 42 top-level entries and 75 named sub-designs — plus 131 idea-engine entries under one parent. The tracking manifest (`pm-stack/design-tracking-manifest-2026-11-11.md`) gives the completed designs that escaped documentation a tracking issue and a committed living document: 37 tracking issues filed, 27 doc files committed. The lineage manifest (`pm-stack/lineage-manifest-2026-11-12.md`) links every issue to epics, boards, and labels. The reprioritization record (`pm-stack/design-reprioritization-2026-11-12.md`) then orders all of it into nine programs with queue bands. This chapter synthesises those four documents and the PM store plans into one roadmap. Statuses quoted from the register are the register's own, live-checked where the register marks them, on 2026-11-11; they are a dated record, not a current census.

The register's status vocabulary is exact and this chapter keeps it. A design is `none`, `drafted`, `decided`, or `built-into-running-system`. Its implementation is `not started`, `partial`, `shipped`, `shipped-then-superseded`, or `abandoned-in-record`. A design recorded as decided is not recorded as built, and this chapter does not promote it.

## The ordering: nine programs, one band map

The reprioritization record of 2026-11-12 is the programme order for the whole estate, and the PM stack sits inside it as program P5. The order, top to bottom:

1. **Drew's expedite set** — pinned, untouched by the program. Nothing in the scheme demotes an expedite item.
2. **Band 10** — fleet-stalling breakage only.
3. **The nine focus programs in Drew's listing order** — P1 ID, P2 Atlas, and P3 Fleet at band 20; P4 SaaS service, **P5 PM stack**, and P6 Turso and Cloudflare at band 30; P7 GitHub and dynamic compute, P8 Storage, and P9 Lab at band 40. Band 50 is reserved and unused by this program.
4. **Blocking small fixes** — a small fix takes the band of the thing it blocks, default band 30 when the blocked thing is unclassifiable.
5. **Non-blocking small fixes** — band 70.
6. **Not-a-work-items** — records, questions, and tracking shells keep their priority; the census notes why.

The census behind the scheme classified 1,227 open issues one by one (1,039 in `developing-today/code`, 188 across the other repos); 115 code issues classified to P5, the PM stack. Application was verified by post-apply re-reads: program epic labels, milestone assignments, and board placement were applied in the census pass. The one leg that did not apply in that pass — queue re-banding, because no band-mutation route was confirmable — closed later the same day: the queue lane applied 392 task re-bands by the established direct-SQL route, each verified by re-read, and the record marks the program's one open gap CLOSED. Priority as recorded still lives in more places than the band: Project fields, rank labels, and the console scale are separate records, and the surfaces do not automatically agree. See [Prioritisation: how order is decided and recorded](../concepts/prioritisation.md).

The lineage manifest supplies the structure the order runs on. An epic is an issue carrying the `epic` label; membership is two-sided — the member carries the epic's `epic/&lt;name&gt;` label and, where the epic issue is unambiguous, the member is a native sub-issue of the epic. Project #4's `Kind` field is the canonical epic field the aggregator reads: `Kind = Epic` makes an epic, and no label substitutes for it (D10). The manifest's amendment pass created the zero-carrier program epics — among them #8986, the design-register epic (27 members, 26 natively linked) — and recorded the labels whose home epic is ambiguous, including `epic/pm-stack` and `epic/pm-stack-coherence`, as awaiting Drew's pick of a single home. Personal-repo issues cannot be placed on org Project #4; that is a settled constraint from Drew's 2026-11-12 ruling, and their tracking home is labels, milestones, and the register.

## The store line: from inventory to migration

Three documents carry the PM store work in sequence, and the sequence is a stated dependency chain, not a preference:

1. **Inventory and target groups — `pm-stack/SHARED-SCHEMA-MAP.md`, tracking #1744.** The map inventories every aggregator-owned store, the projector database, and the native Kaneo, Grist, and Pad stores, and classifies each as rebuildable or not. Its target groups are `pmp_` projector tables, `agg_` aggregator stores, and the shared entity tables `shared_items`, `shared_links`, `shared_comments`, `shared_events`. One finding governs the rest: `pmp_links` is the only non-rebuildable cross-service relationship in the projector schema, and it carries the registry backup legs for that reason.
2. **Direction change — #3142 supersedes #1744's default.** The map records the supersession in its own header: #1744's one-local-libSQL default is replaced by the #3142 decision — start new PM state in Supabase `muse-dev2`, and keep the Kaneo, Grist, and Pad databases separate unless a supported shared-database mode is proven. The first store group is specified as a versioned action/event contract in `pm-stack/shared-schema/`: `public.agg_actions` owns the aggregator's action records, `public.shared_events` holds their cross-service outcomes, with a paired migration and a disposable-PostgreSQL test.
3. **First stores canonical in Supabase — #6815.** Four stores move under the switch `PM_AGG_STORE_BACKEND=supabase`, with `file` as the dev, test, and rollback backend and the local JSON demoted to a rebuildable cache: actions, activity ingestion, usage snapshots, and the AA last-good document, with comments following into `public.shared_comments`. The sibling stores — freshness, notifications, needs-Drew, decisions, decision events, stack health, item history — are follow-on scope under #3989 and live only as laptop-local files in the map's record.
4. **Canonical app stores — `pm-stack/STORE-MIGRATION-PLAN-6816.md`, parent #3989, rulings #1258.** Step 1 of that plan is inventory and plan only; it moves no data and reconfigures no service. Its targets: Kaneo to Supabase Postgres natively; Grist and Pad to Supabase-backed file-shaped homes. Its fencing rule is absolute: the old writer is fenced before any failover promotion, and no configuration permits two active writers. Its cutover runs in stated order — pre-sync while live; stop the writers Pad, then Grist, then Kaneo; final delta; verify; promote — and steps 1 and 5 run through one driver, `pm-stack/scripts/kaneo-cutover.sh`, whose promote mode refuses unless the fence check reports FENCED and the target's table and row counts equal the source's exactly. Step 2 is the windowed cutover itself. Step 3 is the first isolated laptop-off drill, measured against the #1258 targets the plan records: 4-hour core RTO, 1-hour Kaneo RPO, 24-hour Grist and Pad RPO. Rollback is operator steps until the first window records a run.

A second migration direction now runs beside this line: the Turso-to-D1 programme (#13844) moves `fleet-platform` families — including its `agg_*` telemetry and aggregate families — from Turso to D1. The register already records the unresolved ordering between the two directions for the earlier wave (P1.11); the D1 programme adds a third target to the same estate. No document surveyed states which direction governs a given `agg_*` family where the two records name the same family in different stores. This book records that as a disagreement (D19) and states neither direction as settled for the overlap. [Project Management: prioritised issue map and dependency map](issue-map.md) carries the #13844 programme as its own item.
