## Decision to make

When a note is shared into a board post, what exactly does the post hold, what happens when the note later changes, what do replies attach to, how do backlinks and jump-through work in both directions, and what changes when the note and the board live on different servers?

## Recommendation in one paragraph

**Both views, one reference.** A share is a signed board post carrying a structured `NoteRef` — the note's stable ID plus the exact revision ID that was current when the share was made (the *pin*). The post renders, by default, the **live** note (the head resolved in the board's view) with the pin always one step away and visibly badged when live and pinned differ; the pin is the immutable citation of record. Replies anchor to the pin (a revision, and optionally a passage inside it), never to the moving live head. Backlinks are the derived inverse projection defined by #2048, extended with share and reply-anchor edges, filtered by what the viewer may read. Cross-server, a reference is by stable ID and content hash only — sharing copies nothing, grants nothing, and names no host as canonical; a server that cannot obtain the note renders the pin's citation metadata and an explicit unavailable state, never leaked content and never a silent substitute.

This resolves the parent #5677 open question — "live reference, pinned version, or both" — as **both**. That is a completion of a question the parent explicitly left open, not a change to a parent decision (see "Relation to parent decisions").

## The sharing model

### 1. The share object

- Sharing a note into a board creates a signed board `Post` (per #2054's vocabulary) whose body carries a `NoteRef`: `{note_id, pinned_revision_id, board_id, sharer_key, shared_at_event}`. The `NoteRef` is data in the signed body, not a URL, not a host pointer, and not a copy of the note's content.
- The share event is immutable. Editing the post around the embed creates a signed superseding post version per #2054; the `NoteRef` itself changes only by a new share — a post cannot be quietly re-pointed at a different note or a different pin.
- Unsharing is a new signed tombstone/withdrawal event by the sharer (or a board moderation action at the projection layer, per #2054). It never deletes the share event, the note, or replies already anchored to the pin; the board's default view may stop rendering the embed, and the audit history still attributes the withdrawal.
- Who may share: anyone who can read the note may share a reference to it into a board they may post to. This follows the #5677 sovereignty rule — visibility is the gate, not ownership. Sharing grants no rights: it does not make the note editable by board members, does not transfer ownership, and does not widen the note's read ACL. A fork is shared under the fork's own note ID, as its own share; it never masquerades as the original.
- If the note or the board collection has the #5677 draft→approval flow enabled, a share into that scope lands as a pending (draft) post until approved, exactly as any other post in that scope would. The sharing model adds no second approval path.

### 2. Version semantics

- **Pin (citation of record).** `pinned_revision_id` names one immutable signed revision (#2048). The pinned view never changes, never 404s while any replica holds the revision, and is what exports, quotes, and reply anchors cite.
- **Live (reading view).** The default render resolves the note's current head *in the board's view*: the same writer-set/projection rules and multi-head handling as #2048. If the note has diverged into concurrent heads, the live view shows the head the board's view counts and badges the divergence — it never picks a winner by arrival order and never hides the other heads from a reader who follows the jump-through to the note.
- **Drift is signalled, never silent.** When live ≠ pin, the post shows "note updated since shared" with the pin's date/revision short-ID and a one-step toggle to the pinned view. When the pin is *not* an ancestor of the live head (the note was forked or its lineage was superseded), the badge says so explicitly ("shared version is on a superseded line") instead of presenting the fork's content as the shared note's continuation.
- **What an edit to the note does and does not do.** A new revision changes the live view only. It does not rewrite the post, the pin, any reply, or any backlink. A reader who needs to know what the discussion was about always has the pin.
- **Post-side edits.** A superseding post version keeps the original pin unless the superseding version is itself a fresh share event restating the `NoteRef`; ordinary text edits around the embed cannot move the pin.

### 3. Reply anchoring

- A reply is a board object (per #2054) whose anchor is `{note_id, revision_id, passage?}` where `revision_id` defaults to the post's pin. Replies anchor to a *revision*, never to "the note" in the abstract and never to the live head.
- A passage anchor names a stable block/section identifier inside that revision, plus a short quoted-text fallback for human recognition. Block identity follows the note revision's structure (#2048 revisions are immutable snapshots, so a block ID inside a named revision is stable by construction).
- **When the note changes under a discussion:** nothing re-anchors. The reply keeps pointing at the pinned passage. A client *may* offer "show this passage in the current head": it resolves the block ID in the live head if that block survives there, and otherwise reports **anchor drifted** and displays the pinned passage as context. It must never silently re-point the reply at different text that merely looks similar; fuzzy re-anchoring is a display suggestion a human can accept, never a rewrite of the stored anchor.
- A reply may explicitly re-anchor to a newer revision (a new signed statement by the reply's author: "continuing this on revision N"). That is an edit to the reply's anchor recorded in the reply's own history, not a system migration.
- Replies to the post that are not about the note (ordinary thread discussion) need no anchor; anchoring is required only for the structurally-about-the-note references the parent design calls for.

### 4. Backlinks and jump-through

- Backlinks are a derived, rebuildable inverse projection — not stored facts — exactly as #2048 defines for note↔note references, extended with two more edge types: `shared-into` (note → post/board/thread) and `reply-anchored-to` (note revision → reply). The projection is per-store and per-view; replicas holding the same signed event set derive the same backlinks for the same view.
- From a note: every board conversation that shares it or anchors replies to it, listed with the board, thread, pin state (current / drifted / superseded), and the referencing revision.
- From a post or reply: jump-through to the note (live view) and to the exact pinned revision.
- **Access filtering is part of the projection.** A backlink entry is shown only if the viewer may read the referencing conversation. A viewer who may read the note but not a board sees no entry, no title, and no count for that board — backlinks must not become an existence oracle for content the viewer cannot read (the #2048 disclosure invariant, applied unchanged). The note's owner, who can read their own note's full reference graph under their own grants, is not an exception channel for other people's private boards: the same viewer-relative rule applies.
- Chat threads (#2051) that reference a note appear in the same backlink list as a distinct conversation type; whether chat and board threads unify as one conversation type stays with the parent #5677 decomposition item that owns it — this record does not decide it.

### 5. The cross-server case

- A `NoteRef` is resolvable on any server because it names stable IDs and a content-hashed revision, not a host. No server is the canonical home of the note or of the board; convergence is over signed event sets, per #2048 and #2054.
- **Replication follows grants, not shares.** A board's server holding a share does not entitle it to the note's bytes. It obtains the note's revisions only through the note's own read authority — Drew's Q3 ruling as recorded on #2048: default public read, owner ACL where set, precedence object → user default → server config → public. A public note replicates freely to any willing host; an ACL-restricted note replicates only where its grant reaches.
- **Degraded rendering, honestly labelled.** If a server cannot obtain the pinned revision (no grant, no peer has it, note is on an unreachable peer), the post renders the citation metadata it does hold (note ID, pin ID, sharer, authored title surface if present in the share body) plus an explicit "content not available on this server" state. It must not render a different revision as if it were the pin, must not leak content through the backlink list, and must upgrade in place when the revision later arrives.
- **Replies across servers.** A reply anchored to a revision the replying server has not yet seen is stored as-is; its anchor state is `unresolved` until that revision arrives, then it is re-evaluated (exact / drifted) from the stored anchor alone. Ordering of arrival changes nothing in the final projection.
- **Forks across servers** are ordinary forks under sovereignty: a reader on another server who can see the note may fork it; the fork lives under its own note ID, and its shares are new shares. Cross-server marks of "most-correct" remain per-community signals (#5677), never a global flag one server can set for another.
- **Withdrawal across servers** is a projection matter per server and board view: the withdrawal event propagates like any other signed event, hosts apply their own availability policy (#2054), and no host can compel another to erase the share event or the note's history.

## Alternatives rejected

| Option | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **Snapshot copy** — paste the note's content into the post at share time | Severs provenance: the copy diverges silently, backlinks cannot be derived to a copy that claims no identity, forks of the copy are invisible to the original, and the "copy" contradicts #2048's stable-identity model. It is the failure the parent's "live reference, not a pasted copy" wording exists to prevent. |
| **Live-only reference, no pin** | Citation rot: replies and discussion lose their referent the first time the note is edited; "what was shared" becomes unanswerable; moderation and audit cannot reconstruct what a board actually displayed. |
| **Pin-only reference, no live view** | Safe but dead: boards become archives of stale snapshots, readers must manually re-share to follow a living note, and the connective surface the parent asks for (notes and boards as one knowledge surface) collapses into cross-posting with extra steps. |
| **Host-mediated embed** (URL/iframe-style embed served by the note's "home" server) | Makes one host canonical and online-required; breaks offline and terminal parity; the serving host can alter or withdraw what every board displays, contradicting both #2054's host-independence and the sovereignty rule. |
| **Copy the note into board ownership on share** | Transfers effective control to the board, forbids the author from continuing their own note as the shared object, and directly violates sovereignty (visibility gates forking; ownership is not reassigned by being referenced). |
| **System auto-re-anchoring of replies on edit** (fuzzy text matching) | Silently rewrites what a reply was about; a single bad match falsifies a discussion record. Drift must be surfaced, not papered over. |

## Invariants implementation must hold

1. A share is a signed post carrying `{note_id, pinned_revision_id}`; the pin never changes except by a new share event.
2. The pinned revision renders byte-identically forever; the live view is a projection and is always labelled when it differs from the pin.
3. Divergence is never hidden: multi-head notes and superseded pins badge explicitly; no arrival-order or wall-clock winner is ever selected silently.
4. Replies anchor to revisions (and optionally stable blocks within them); anchors are never rewritten by the system — drift is reported, re-anchoring is an authored act.
5. Backlinks are derived, rebuildable, and viewer-filtered: no backlink discloses the existence of a conversation the viewer may not read.
6. Sharing grants nothing and transfers nothing: no read, write, or ownership right over a note is created, widened, or moved by a share.
7. References are host-independent: stable IDs and content hashes only; no URL, endpoint, or home server is part of a reference's identity.
8. Unavailable content degrades honestly: citation metadata + explicit unavailable state; never a substitute revision presented as the pin, never leaked content.
9. Withdrawal and moderation act on projections, not history: the share event, the note's revisions, and anchored replies remain attributable in the audit record.
10. Parity: shares, pins, anchors, and backlinks have identical semantics on human, CLI/script, and agent surfaces (the parent's graph-parity requirement).

## Implementation splits (filed/linked from #5830, not built here)

1. `NoteRef` share object: post-body schema carrying the reference, share/withdraw events, pin rendering and drift badging.
2. Live-view resolution for embeds: board-view head resolution with multi-head and superseded-line badging, reusing the #2048 revision projections.
3. Reply anchors: anchor schema, passage/block addressing inside a revision, drift detection against descendant revisions.
4. Backlink projection extension: `shared-into` and `reply-anchored-to` edge types with viewer-relative access filtering.
5. Cross-server embed fetch: grant-checked revision replication for embeds and the honest degraded-rendering state.

Each split is a separate issue when implementation is scheduled; this record is their shared decision basis and none of them may re-decide §§1–5 without amending this record explicitly.
