## The workstream order

The list gives the proposed working order for the retrieval and integration programme. The issue register below distinguishes recorded dependencies from recommended sequencing. Evaluation applies throughout.

1. **Ingest and freshness first.** Automatic ingest of all sources ([Design: automatic ingest of all sources](automatic-ingest.md)) — continuous ingestion, the ingest watermark, and a verified corpus recount. Record the corpus generation used by serving, evaluation and each result cache. The October publish provides a historical comparison. Freshness is also the fleet-facing fix (see [Design: search integration for fleet and ID](fleet-and-id-integration.md)).
2. **Query planning and layered search characteristics.** The planner the API already pretends to have — routing, filter extraction, conversational rewriting first; expansion, multi-query, HyDE, and decomposition second ([Design: query planning](query-planning.md)) — together with the layered signals on the Snowflake base: learned sparse (SPLADE), late interaction (ColBERT) as a mid-stage, metadata pre-filtering, and the ANN serving path that replaces the ~4-second exact scan ([Design: layered search characteristics on Snowflake](layered-search.md)). The second-embedder evaluation ([#9092](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9092)) is decided in this workstream, on eval results.
3. **Reranking.** A cross-encoder stage over the fused candidate pool ([Design: the reranking stage](reranking.md)), adopted per corpus and golden-set-gated. It comes after the layered work because it ranks what the layers retrieve: reranking a weak candidate pool polishes a recall failure.
4. **The recommender.** The single-user recommender ([Design: the single-user recommender](recommender.md)) — profile-centroid and session co-occurrence candidates, ranked and diversity-re-ranked — builds on retrieval and the [merged snapshot-paging foundation](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/12873). Candidate generation and serving remain separate acceptance steps.
5. **Fleet and ID integration.** Result caching keyed by index version, batch search, embedded-replica offline fallback, and search-quality telemetry for the fleet; the #6062 publication format, the #11182–#11186 projectors, and #9109 Part C for ID ([Design: search integration for fleet and ID](fleet-and-id-integration.md)). Integration follows the retrieval work so that what is integrated is the finished core, not an interim one.
6. **Access growth.** The MCP tool growth, REST `explain`, the grown `atlas-proto` contract, and #8625's cross-client acceptance ([Design: MCP and programmatic access, next steps](access-evolution.md)). Access is last because each new surface freezes a shape; the shapes are frozen after the core settles.

**Gated on the evaluation programme, throughout.** [Design: the standing evaluation programme](evaluation-programme.md) is not a seventh workstream at the end of the list. It is the gate across all six: every model, chunker, fusion, reranker, and planner change passes its CI regression gate, and its golden set grows from the real traces the other workstreams produce. A workstream that cannot show its eval result has not finished, whatever its code state.

## Decisions and dependencies

Blockers are marked here as they stood at the 2026-11-23 count. A blocker is a named issue or decision that stops named work; flag-held means the work may not start without the owner's item-specific word, and shepherd-held means a finished PR waits in the parent programme's shepherd queue and is not merged by the programme's own lanes.

- **D1 cache.** [Invalidation](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/13378) merged under the existing master flag. The [read-path issue](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13105) names freshness and admission as prerequisites. [Measurement](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13107) needs read-path telemetry. Confirm deployment and flag state separately.
- **[#8479](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/8479) — purge versus reclassify is the user's decision.** The 638 secret-bearing chunks also live in the production monolith and a local replica, so the tier boundary does not currently hold outside the shard set. The options are a purge from the wrong-tier stores or a reclassification of the material; the destructive leg is additionally gated on production-ingestion verification. No programme work decides this, and no claim that the tier boundary holds may be made until it is decided and done.
- **[#12900](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12900) thread 2 — the user's decision.** Whether Atlas is headed for multi-client use or stays the user's personal system. Thread 1 (parallel branches) landed via PR #13039; thread 3 (the Nix-packaged binary) substantially landed elsewhere; thread 2 stays open as a product question. It is presented as an open decision in [Design: MCP and programmatic access, next steps](access-evolution.md) and is not designed around.
- **The thinking-traces question — open.** Inside [#9097](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9097) item 4 (all OpenCode sessions): whether to ingest thinking traces, with a recommendation covering volume, value, and classification implications. Undecided; the investigation is filed, the answer is the user's.
- **Ingestion.** [Workstream E](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9097) still needs continuous source discovery and reconciliation. The [single-unit aggregator](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/10832), [promotion gate](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/11995) and [run-once sweep](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/11210) are implementation foundations. External GitHub intake waits on licensing and zone policy.
- **Recommendation and cleanup work.** [Snapshot paging](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/12873) and the [durable accumulator](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/13353) merged. Candidate generation, the signer and production binding need separate evidence. The [clippy PR](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/13097) remains subject to its item-specific hold.
- **Also standing:** the atlas-core clippy issue [#13056](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13056) itself (open, in review, 22 pre-existing warnings recorded); the Worker cache issue [#6805](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/6805) (open; the fix is designed in the access chapter); and the secret-tier follow-ons [#12403](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12403), [#12404](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12404), and [#12406](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12406) on the #8479 line.
