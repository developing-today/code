## The workstream order

The programme runs in one order, for one reason: each workstream's output is the next workstream's input, and the evaluation programme gates all of them. The order is the master prompt's §2 sequence, restated as a dependency chain.

1. **Ingest and freshness first.** Automatic ingest of all sources ([Design: automatic ingest of all sources](automatic-ingest.md)) — continuous ingestion, the ingest watermark, and a verified corpus recount. Everything downstream is measured against, cached against, and served from the corpus; while the corpus is frozen at the 2026-10-31 publish, every later workstream is building on stale ground. Freshness is also the fleet-facing fix (see [Design: search integration for fleet and ID](fleet-and-id-integration.md)).
2. **Query planning and layered search characteristics.** The planner the API already pretends to have — routing, filter extraction, conversational rewriting first; expansion, multi-query, HyDE, and decomposition second ([Design: query planning](query-planning.md)) — together with the layered signals on the Snowflake base: learned sparse (SPLADE), late interaction (ColBERT) as a mid-stage, metadata pre-filtering, and the ANN serving path that replaces the ~4-second exact scan ([Design: layered search characteristics on Snowflake](layered-search.md)). The second-embedder evaluation ([#9092](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9092)) is decided in this workstream, on eval results.
3. **Reranking.** A cross-encoder stage over the fused candidate pool ([Design: the reranking stage](reranking.md)), adopted per corpus and golden-set-gated. It comes after the layered work because it ranks what the layers retrieve: reranking a weak candidate pool polishes a recall failure.
4. **The recommender.** The single-user recommender ([Design: the single-user recommender](recommender.md)) — profile-centroid and session co-occurrence candidates, ranked and diversity-re-ranked — built on the retrieval stack the previous workstreams finished, and on its snapshot-paging foundation ([#10158](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/10158), whose PR is flag-held; see Blockers).
5. **Fleet and ID integration.** Result caching keyed by index version, batch search, embedded-replica offline fallback, and search-quality telemetry for the fleet; the #6062 publication format, the #11182–#11186 projectors, and #9109 Part C for ID ([Design: search integration for fleet and ID](fleet-and-id-integration.md)). Integration follows the retrieval work so that what is integrated is the finished core, not an interim one.
6. **Access growth.** The MCP tool growth, REST `explain`, the grown `atlas-proto` contract, and #8625's cross-client acceptance ([Design: MCP and programmatic access, next steps](access-evolution.md)). Access is last because each new surface freezes a shape; the shapes are frozen after the core settles.

**Gated on the evaluation programme, throughout.** [Design: the standing evaluation programme](evaluation-programme.md) is not a seventh workstream at the end of the list. It is the gate across all six: every model, chunker, fusion, reranker, and planner change passes its CI regression gate, and its golden set grows from the real traces the other workstreams produce. A workstream that cannot show its eval result has not finished, whatever its code state.

## Standing blockers

Blockers are marked here as they stood at the 2026-11-23 count. A blocker is a named issue or decision that stops named work; flag-held means the work may not start without Drew's item-specific word, and shepherd-held means a finished PR waits in the parent programme's shepherd queue and is not merged by the programme's own lanes.

- **The D1 chain ([#9134](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9134)).** The D1 tenant hot-cache chain: [#13100](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13100) (schema and store) is closed and landed, with the master flag off. [#13101](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13101) (freshness evaluator) and [#13103](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13103) (admission policy) are open and in review. **[#13105](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13105) (read-path integration) is gated on #13101** — it may not proceed until the freshness evaluator lands. **[#13106](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13106) (invalidation) is flag-held** — it awaits Drew's item-specific go-ahead and is not ordinary queue work. [#13107](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13107) (measurement report) sits behind #13105. Net effect: production search does not touch D1 today, and the chain advances only through #13101.
- **[#8479](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/8479) — purge versus reclassify is Drew's decision.** The 638 secret-bearing chunks also live in the production monolith and a local replica, so the tier boundary does not currently hold outside the shard set. The options are a purge from the wrong-tier stores or a reclassification of the material; the destructive leg is additionally gated on production-ingestion verification. No programme work decides this, and no claim that the tier boundary holds may be made until it is decided and done.
- **[#12900](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12900) thread 2 — Drew's decision.** Whether Atlas is headed for multi-client use or stays Drew's personal system. Thread 1 (parallel branches) landed via PR #13039; thread 3 (the Nix-packaged binary) substantially landed elsewhere; thread 2 stays open as a product question. It is presented as an open decision in [Design: MCP and programmatic access, next steps](access-evolution.md) and is not designed around.
- **The thinking-traces question — open.** Inside [#9097](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9097) item 4 (all OpenCode sessions): whether to ingest thinking traces, with a recommendation covering volume, value, and classification implications. Undecided; the investigation is filed, the answer is Drew's.
- **[#9097](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/9097) Workstream E — blocked.** Continuous ingestion of the queue/PM, GitHub, Muse, OpenCode, and ID corpora plus the sources-inventory pass. It is the programme's first workstream and it is blocked: the staging tier and promote gate exist in code, but the aggregator/embedder, the session-close hook, and the recurring sweep runner ([#7969](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/7969), open) are not built, and its external-GitHub half waits on the licensing and zone-policy gates ([#6024](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/6024), [#6052](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/6052), both open).
- **Flag-held shepherds: PR #12873 and PR #13097.** PR [#12873](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/12873) (recommendation snapshot paging, [#10158](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/10158)) and PR [#13097](https://github.com/developing-today/code/pull/13097) (atlas-core clippy cleanup, [#13056](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13056)) are both open and unmerged, held in the shepherd queue. They are not merged without Drew's word, and work depending on them — the recommender's paging foundation, in #12873's case — plans around the hold rather than through it.
- **Also standing:** the atlas-core clippy issue [#13056](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13056) itself (open, in review, 22 pre-existing warnings recorded); the Worker cache issue [#6805](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/6805) (open; the fix is designed in the access chapter); and the secret-tier follow-ons [#12403](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12403), [#12404](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12404), and [#12406](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/12406) on the #8479 line.

## What exists today

- The programme exists as a labelled issue set and an epic: 57 open `epic/atlas-program` issues at the 2026-11-23 count, epic [#8983](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/8983) open, and the full-investigation tracking issue [#13241](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13241) as the living map. This book is tracked separately as [#13251](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13251).
- Shipped today, beneath the roadmap: the hybrid search core (FTS + vector, RRF fusion only — no reranker in the serving path), the Snowflake Arctic Embed m-v2.0 embedder at 768 dimensions with bit-identical Rust/Python parity, `fleet search` and the `atlas-search` CLI, the laptop search service, the Cloudflare Worker with its single-tool MCP server, the golden-311 evaluation harness, staging with a promote gate, and the #3125 provenance phases 1–2 (12,092 content-addressed objects interned, verified 2026-11-23).
- Not shipped today, and therefore roadmap rather than record: continuous ingest, the query planner (its contract flags are accepted and refused as unimplemented), any learned-sparse, late-interaction, ANN, or reranking stage in the serving path, the recommender beyond its design and one held paging PR, any ID query path, per-agent MCP identity, and any D1 read path (the D1 master flag is off and the store is unwired in the request path).
- The blockers listed above stood as described at the 2026-11-23 count: #13105 gated on #13101, #13106 flag-held, #8479 and #12900 thread 2 awaiting Drew's decisions, the thinking-traces question open, #9097 blocked, and PRs #12873 and #13097 flag-held in the shepherd queue.
