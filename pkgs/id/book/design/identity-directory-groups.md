# Design: identity, directory, groups and delegation

This chapter covers 56 design records, the largest design theme in the book after the knowledge layer. It describes identity, the directory, groups and delegation as designed in the programme's design archive. The final section states what runs today.

The thread runs through the core epic #2056, Phase 2 (membership, auth frontier and delegation) and Phase 4 (groups, encryption and history) of the phase records, and a set of named trackers: #2380 (keyset authorization log), #2382 (guardian recovery), #3231/#3232 (the receipt contract and its audit), #8750 (block-list lookups), #9141 (peer trust classes) and the federation cluster #9129–#9148.

## Accounts, groups, contacts and delegation

The founding record is `2026-10-09T12-00-00Z_feature_directory_accounts_groups` (2026-10-09): accounts, groups, contacts and delegation as one feature design. The directory is the programme's answer to a concrete question: how does a node know who someone is, which groups they belong to, and what they may do, without a central account server.

The largest single record in the archive belongs to this chapter: `2026-10-11T03-44-33Z_design_groups_events_delegation` (2026-10-11), a 407-file bundle covering events, groups, delegation, removal and history, together with a connectivity record and relay, range and BeeKEM spike code, logs and certificates. Group membership in this design is event-sourced: membership changes are signed events, removal is an event with a history, and delegation is a signed narrowing of authority. The removal semantics are made explicit in `2026-11-02T04-44-00Z_design_group_remove_modes` (2026-11-02), "Explicit group removal modes": removal has named modes instead of one ambiguous remove.

`2026-10-10T07-00-00Z_feature_petnames_invites` (2026-10-10) designs petnames and invite links: local names for people, assigned by the namer, plus invites as the entry path into groups and worlds. Petname safety gets its own record: `2026-11-02T12-47-00Z_design_petname_unicode_confusable_skeleton` (2026-11-02) designs petname anti-mimicry using the Unicode UTS #39 confusable skeleton, so a look-alike name is detected by its skeleton, not by eye. Invites get a second, deeper design in `2026-11-14T18-50-00Z_design_id_invite_delegation` (2026-11-14): generic invite delegation — encoding, depth, leakage, and group live-set semantics. Invite depth is a designed quantity: an invite can be re-delegated only within its stated depth, and the record treats information leakage through invites as a design constraint.

Worlds consume the directory directly. `2026-10-17T08-00-00Z_feature_world_roles_directory` (2026-10-17) designs worlds as places, first slice: role-scoped invites and a machine-readable world list. `2026-11-01_design_id835.md` (2026-11-01) designs builder and moderator authority through world-session frames (#835, roles-v2 and moderator wiring under Phase 12). `2026-10-18T04-00-00Z_fix_world_chat_visibility` (2026-10-18) records the visibility rule as a fix: world chat is visible only to participants who may chat or read history. `2026-10-16T17-00-00Z_feature_chat_history` (2026-10-16) designs server-observed chat history and grantable platform modules.

## Signed artifacts, home servers and trust

`2026-10-10T04-58-36Z_design_signed_artifacts` (2026-10-10) designs signed artifacts, home servers, and cross-world access: a person's home server holds and serves their signed artifacts, and other worlds accept them on the signature. The trust model that governs acceptance is investigated in `2026-10-30T19-11-04Z_investigation_trust_visibility_model` (2026-10-30): accept-all ingest with writer-set views — ingest accepts, and visibility is a projection over writer sets. The writer-set design itself is in [Design: sync, replication and FileOps](sync-fileops.md).

Module trust is an identity question in this design: `2026-11-02T00-31-45Z_design_module_trust` (2026-11-02) designs the admin trust list and the install-time signature check. Its runtime side is cross-linked from [Design: worlds and the guest runtime](worlds-runtime.md).

## What exists today

Grounded in the code surveys, not the design records:

- The directory ships as an append-only journal: `directory.jsonl`, one JSON entry per line, replayed through the same apply path as live changes, with 17 entry kinds covering account signup, keying, verification, key add and remove, email confirm and remove, group create, update, visibility and delete, member set, tier set, and the friend request, accept, remove and removal-noted kinds. Levels are `access`, `read`, `write`, `manage`, `moderator` and `admin`; tiers are `anonymous`, `registered` and `verified`. Account secrets enter the journal only as SHA-256 digests.
- Signed artifacts ship in a legacy profile with a pinned format: signed bytes are compact JSON under the profile name `id-artifact-serde-json/v1`, hashed with SHA-256 and signed with Ed25519, in four kinds (`friend_remove`, `home_declared`, `document`, `tag`). Test vectors for the profile ship in `pkgs/id/test-vectors/`.
- Keysets ship as JSONL files (`.id-keyset.jsonl` and `.id-keyset-client.jsonl`), invites carry the magic string `IDINV` at version 2, and the key envelope carries the magic string `IDKEY`.
- Server provisioning ships in the server config: users and groups can be declared in TOML and are provisioned at startup, idempotently, with group member roles `pull`, `read`, `write`, `manage` and `admin`.
- Still design only, with no shipped counterpart verified in the surveys: guardian social recovery (#2382) as a running flow, the cross-signed delegation ceremony of 2026-11-21, and moderation packs as a distributed format. The surveys verify the journal, artifact and keyset machinery; the recovery and governance layers on top of it remain design. Individual record-to-code correspondence beyond the items above is unverified in the survey material.
