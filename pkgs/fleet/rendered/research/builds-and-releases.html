Convergence across the forks

In the November 23 historical sample, twelve reporting issues itemized 118 failed target attempts over 102 distinct forks and eleven distinct source SHAs. The classifications were 100 failures from missing workflow scope, 15 rule refusals on two forks and three conflicts on one fork. A thirteenth failed workflow could not post its usual report. These denominators describe recorded failures; a fleet-wide failure rate also needs successful attempts in the same window. Issue closure time measures triage. The ancestry test measures convergence. A transient network failure gets bounded backoff within the rate budget. A merge conflict requires target-specific review that preserves the fork’s commits. Membership and release acceptance

At umbrella revision 45ad3e42, the policy and submodule declarations contained 22 unique matching path/repository pairs. The management tiers comprise one FULL member, four FULL-lite members and seventeen umbrella/autocommit members. The member table records the individual purpose and policy. Devcontainer initialization, warm builds, requested permissions and membership have separate consumers. Never infer access or initialization from a gitlink. Compare the policy, declared path/URL and actual mode-160000 tree entry at one commit. Before adding a member, record its purpose, owner, consumers, tier and removal path. When removing it, unwind consumers and indexing policy before removing the gitlink. Keep private member contents outside the public umbrella.
