High-level roadmap

This chapter is Fleet’s section of the whole-project priority list: the merged census of record for the Code Book programme. The full merged list, ordered across the whole project, lives in the Code Book; the programme is tracked on #13905. The ordering logic is stated once there and summarised here: execution substrate first (nothing else lands without a trustworthy queue, build capacity, and transport), then the physical substrate, then the data substrate, then search intelligence, then the ID platform build-out, then the gateway, PM and fork surfaces, then the record itself. Within a tier, dependency order wins; ties break on leverage.

Four items are Fleet’s own and are given in full below. The remaining Fleet-assigned items belong primarily to another book; they appear as one-line entries at the end, each linking to the primary book’s high-level roadmap chapter. This chapter cross-references the Fleet research and implementation roadmap, which carries the engineering order and acceptance evidence, and the Fleet issue and blocker map, which carries issue-by-issue membership; it does not duplicate either. Statuses are as of the 2026-11-23 census unless a later read is named. Timing is taken from the census record: where the record has no timing, this chapter says so rather than projecting a date.

1. Fleet queue, claim and placement correctness

  • Type: problem.
  • Description: The queue broker, claim/renewal fencing, packet repair and placement must be provably correct — every other programme’s work is dispatched through them.
  • Status: in flight, substantially landed. The repair verb shipped (PR #13218), with a production drain of 37 repairs on broker generation 6 recorded on #13191; the double-serve defect (#13091) is fixed (PR #13143 merged); #13146 placement improvement closed after criterion 1 was proven cross-seat on 2026-11-23. The Fleet research and implementation roadmap puts “reconcile the production repair cohort” first, ahead of any queue-policy change.
  • Most related: #13272 fleet programme tracker · #13191 repair verb for quarantined packets · #13091 double-serve defect · #13146 placement improvement · #4986 placement framework v1 · #12236 every lane on the intake ladder · roadmap order 1–6 in the Fleet research and implementation roadmap.
  • Timing: entered the record as the fleet programme #13272, 2026-11-23. The repair-verb production drain and the generation-6 broker state are dated 2026-11-23 in the programme roadmap. Nothing was planned against a completion date in the record, and nothing was missed against one; what came in during the window was the repair cohort itself, which reprioritised this item’s interior — reconcile the production repair cohort and inspect renewal arithmetic before changing queue policy.
  • Ordering rationale: dependency root of the whole project: a queue that double-serves or loses packets silently corrupts every other item’s evidence. Highest leverage in the census.

2. Containerised build/compile systems — in the cluster and in Actions

  • Type: capability-set.
  • Description: Builds and compiles run in containers with a central coordinator — atomic slots, a true load view, global budgets — plus live short-lived exec sessions inside Actions runners, so no build depends on one laptop.
  • Status: designed + in flight. The compile coordinator is designed and its first packet is parked on the flag route; actions-exec was actively building on 2026-11-23, with slices landing through 22:10Z. The in-cluster half is not started — it waits on the Talos cluster (cross-linked item 6 below).
  • Most related: #13765 compile coordinator (already tasked — task 21517 parked, flag-routed to a laptop OpenCode session per the ID-100 record) · #13774 actions-exec · #13844 Turso→D1 audit’s exec-door family #13404/#13657 · stream F (build and test: mirror parity, image/cache identity) in the Fleet research and implementation roadmap.
  • Timing: #13765 and #13774 were both filed 2026-11-23 (21:14Z / 21:21Z); actions-exec slices were landing through 22:10Z the same day. No projected dates in record; no miss recorded — the in-cluster half is sequenced, not late: it waits on item 6.
  • Ordering rationale: build capacity is the second bottleneck after the queue; the Actions half can proceed now, the cluster half sequences behind the cluster itself.

  • Status: designed / investigation open. Both programme issues were filed 2026-11-23 and are open; the laptop-agent fallback is partially landed (SSH leg PR’d 2026-11-22).
  • Most related: #13752 fleet transport & pooling · #13779 capability auth + P2P transport investigation · #10796 laptop-agent domain: automatic transport fallback · mcpx transports incl. #1384 supervisor design.
  • Timing: #13752 filed 2026-11-23T21:11Z; #13779 filed 2026-11-23T21:29Z. No projected dates in record; nothing missed — the item entered the record during the census window itself.
  • Ordering rationale: every dynamic-server, federation and remote-exec ambition in the merged list assumes this answer; settling it early prevents three programmes building three transports.
