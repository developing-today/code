# Costs and monthly usage

This chapter is the light cost record for the estate: the shape of the money, the totals the sources carry, and the latest dated usage reads. It keeps two buckets separate from the first section to the last — plan and included usage in one bucket, pay-as-you-go in the other — and it never mixes them. Every figure carries the date its source was read, in the same sentence or the same table row. No figure in this chapter is current. A figure with an old read date is labelled stale with its age. The plan inventory itself, with its fees and windows, is in [The plans we use](the-plans.md); this chapter does not repeat that detail.

## The shape of the money

The estate's spend comes in two shapes, and the fleet's rules treat them as different kinds of money:

- **Plan and included usage.** Fixed-fee plans carry an included allowance metered in windows — a weekly window, a 5-hour window, a monthly bucket. Work burns the allowance, not cash. When a window reaches 100% the pool is closed until its reset; nothing bills past the wall. The monthly usage table below is this bucket.
- **Pay-as-you-go.** Metered spend against a balance, charged per use. The standing rule for supervised sessions is never funds: a quota or funds error is a wall, never a fallback to a balance (POOL MAP, registry, read 2026-11-23). The dated exceptions to that rule, and the one standing reserve, are in the pay-as-you-go section below — kept separate, as the rule requires.

Turso and Cloudflare Workers sit in the first shape: each is a plan with an included allowance, and their sections below record the plan, the estate's measured usage against it, and nothing finer.

## Turso

The estate's databases sit on Turso's Scaler plan (plan named in the #13844 audit record, read 2026-11-23). The #13844 Turso audit measured the estate on 2026-11-23 (live Turso API inventory read 2026-11-23 at 23:05Z, audit merged through PR #13845): 50 databases, ~65.8M rows written, ~7.47B rows read, and ~10.0 GB stored per period (audit, read 2026-11-23). The settled estate list confirms the count: a live `turso db list` on the laptop on 2026-11-23 returned exactly 50 databases (lane TURSO-SETTLE, 2026-11-23). The full estate is enumerated in [Data stores: the Turso estate](../reference/data-stores.md).

Two stores dominate the two meters. On reads, the audit attributes the estate's read volume to `fleet-queue`, the queue broker's core store: 81% of all reads (6.08B rows read), driven by the broker and its pollers (audit, read 2026-11-23; the read-driver attribution is disagreement D40, and the audit's attribution is the one this book follows). On writes, `fleet-platform`, the PM and dashboard plane store, takes 87% of the estate's writes (coverage ledger Table B, census date 2026-11-23).

The D1 migration's cost logic is one paragraph in the audit's programme ([#13844](https://github.com/developing-today/code/issues/13844)): moving the `fleet-platform` families to Cloudflare D1 removes the estate's dominant write load from Turso, and the audit's downgrade path runs from there — a Turso plan downgrade gated on the programme's G1–G3 work plus the G4 read-shape fix (#13844 issue body, read 2026-11-23). At the audit's own record the programme is research and design only; no migrations have been made (issue-map snapshot, 2026-11-23). This chapter records the path and does not re-argue it.

## Pay-as-you-go, separately

Pay-as-you-go is the second bucket, and it stays fenced. The standing rule is never funds (POOL MAP, read 2026-11-23), and the measured reason for the fence is dated: on 2026-10-25 a Meta plan burn spilled $20.00 plus $3.91, $23.91 in total, onto pay-as-you-go charges, with a third $20.00 charge on 2026-10-26 (registry, read 2026-11-23). Against that standing rule, the sources record one standing reserve and three TEMPORARY tails.

The standing reserve is DeepSeek direct (api.deepseek.com, balance-funded). Its role: the pay-as-you-go reserve for when every plan is used up — never a routine route and never an agent default (registry provider facts, read 2026-11-23). Routine DeepSeek comes from plan routes (ClinePass, Open Design, OpenCode Go). Its one dated balance read in the sources is ¥43.82 (registry cycle 404, dated 2026-11-10) — 13 days stale at this chapter's date (2026-11-23), recorded as visibility only.

The three TEMPORARY tails are Drew's dated exception, ordered 2026-11-23 13:20 CST and TEMPORARY until he lifts the order (standing-rules record, read 2026-11-23). Each tail sits last in its pool, behind every plan and free leg, and exists to catch fall-through traffic only after the included allowances are spent:

- **DeepSeek direct pay-as-you-go** — added last in the DeepSeek pool (order, 2026-11-23). This is the standing reserve door above, seated as a tail for the duration of the order.
- **Claude pay-as-you-go Haiku, low** — added last in the Claude pool (order, 2026-11-23).
- **OpenAI pay-as-you-go Luna, low** — added last in the OpenAI pool (order, 2026-11-23).

No other pay-as-you-go balance is stated in this chapter: the sources date-stamp no balance for the Claude or OpenAI tails, and none is given here.
